\section{\texorpdfstring{The $b$-field: the antisymmetric part of the shift}{The b-field: the antisymmetric part of the shift}}
\label{d4:sec:bfield}

The hypothesis places the $b$-field of Yang and Mills \cite{YangMills54} at the origin of the mass. This section asks where a connection
enters the interior dynamics of Sections~15--17 of \cite{ES17}, and what it can do there. A constant shift of the interior Lagrangian splits
into the $\eta$-self-adjoint binding term $\kappa G$ and an $\eta$-antisymmetric part $b\in\so{3,3}$, and only $b$ is a connection
(\cref{d4:thm:covariant}). A Yang--Mills $b$-field is moreover compact (Theorem~2.3 and Corollary~2.4 of \cite{ES17}), $b=(A+C,A-C)$ on the two
triples. Its diagonal part splits the binding frequency into Zeeman lines that can be gauged away along any worldline
(\cref{d4:thm:lG,d4:prop:larmor}); its anti-diagonal part alone tears the binding (\cref{d4:thm:pG}); together they keep every orbit bounded
exactly when the triples rotate at equal rates about axes that are not opposite, outside one exceptional ratio (\cref{d4:thm:screen}). On its
own $b$ does not bind, and nothing in the interior dynamics ties the mass $m$ to the rate $\kappa$ (\cref{d4:prop:alone}). Units are $\hbar=c=1$
unless a symbol is shown; $\tau$ has dimension $T$, $\kappa=c/\ell$ (equation~(7) of \cite{ES17}) and every eigenvalue of $b$ are rates, and
$\hbar\kappa$ is a quantum of interior energy. Every finite-dimensional statement was checked by \texttt{verify/d4\_bfield.py} and
independently by \texttt{verify/x4\_check.py} (\cref{d4:tab:claims}).

\paragraph{Notation.} For $a<b$, $R_{ab}\in\so{3,3}$ is defined by $R_{ab}e_b=\eta_{bb}e_a$ and $R_{ab}e_a=-\eta_{aa}e_b$; on vectors it equals
$\half\ad(e_ae_b)$. The generators of $\lG$ (Theorems~12.1 and~12.3 of \cite{ES17}) are $L_i=\half\ad(e_{j+3}e_{k+3}-e_je_k)$ and
$K_i=\ad\bigl(\half(e_ke_{j+3}-e_je_{k+3})\bigr)$ for cyclic $(i,j,k)$. On $z=\bB+i\bE$ they act as $z\mapsto\mathbf e_i\times z$ and
$z\mapsto i\,\mathbf e_i\times z$, so $K_i=GL_i$; the $L_i$ are rotations and the $K_i$ boosts, and
\begin{equation}
\label{d4:eq:w}
b=\textstyle\sum_i\bigl(w^{\rm r}_iL_i+w^{\rm b}_iK_i\bigr)\in\lG\quad\text{acts as}\quad z\mapsto w\times z,\qquad w=w^{\rm r}+i\,w^{\rm b}\in\C^3 .
\end{equation}
In particular $R_{12}-R_{45}=-L_3$. The grading root is $\mathcal K=\half\ad\bigl(\sum_ie_ie_{i+3}\bigr)$, with $G=-\mathcal K\eta$
(Lemma~3.4 of \cite{ES17}). We write $\sigma_j$ for the boost exchanging $e_j$ and $e_{j+3}$, $F^\dagger=\eta^{-1}F^{\!\top}\eta$,
$[\mathbf v]_\times$ for the matrix of $\mathbf x\mapsto\mathbf v\times\mathbf x$ on $\R^3$, $\mathcal E_\mu(T)=\ker(T-\mu)$, and
$h(y,y')=\bar y^{\!\top}\eta y'$. The interior Lagrangian with a constant shift is
\begin{equation}
\label{d4:eq:L}
\mathcal L=\frac m2\,\eta(u,u),\qquad u=\dot X+SX,\qquad S=b+\kappa G,\qquad b\in\so{3,3},
\end{equation}
with $p=m\eta u$ and $\pi=\eta^{-1}p=mu$. For $b=0$ this is the split-form Lagrangian of Theorem~16.2 of \cite{ES17}.

\subsection{Two summands, and which one is a connection}
\label{d4:sec:split}

\begin{proposition}[Two summands]
\label{d4:prop:split}
$\End(V)=\so{3,3}\oplus\{F:F=F^\dagger\}$, of dimensions $15+21$. The part of $S=b+\kappa G$ in the first summand is $b$, and the part in the
second is $\kappa G$. The binding root lies in the second summand ($G^\dagger=G$) and the grading root in the first ($\mathcal K^\dagger=-\mathcal K$).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$F\mapsto F^\dagger$ is an involution, and $F$ lies in its $\mp1$ eigenspace exactly when $\eta F$ is antisymmetric (respectively symmetric).
\end{proof}

This restates Theorem~17.3 of \cite{ES17} at $n=1$, and the placement of the two roots is Proposition~3.3 and Theorem~17.3 of \cite{ES17}
(fixed there); the decomposition itself is linear algebra valid for any nondegenerate form. The next theorem gives it dynamical meaning.

\begin{theorem}[Covariant factorisation]
\label{d4:thm:covariant}
Let $b\in\so{3,3}$ be constant and $D:=\frac{d}{d\tau}+b$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item The Euler--Lagrange equations of \eqref{d4:eq:L} are $(D-\kappa G)\pi=0$ with $\pi=m(D+\kappa G)X$, that is
\begin{equation}
\label{d4:eq:covEL}
(D-\kappa G)(D+\kappa G)X=0\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad D^2X+\kappa^2X+\kappa\,(D_\tau G)\,X=0,\qquad D_\tau G:=[b,G].
\end{equation}
\item Let $X=g(\tau)Y$ with $g(\tau)\in O(3,3)$. Then $\bigl(\tfrac d{d\tau}+S\bigr)(gY)=g\bigl(\tfrac d{d\tau}+S^g\bigr)Y$, where $S^g=g^{-1}Sg+g^{-1}\dot g$. The
$\so{3,3}$ part $b^g=g^{-1}bg+g^{-1}\dot g$ transforms as a connection, the self-adjoint part $\kappa g^{-1}Gg$ as a tensor, and
$(D_\tau G)^g=g^{-1}(D_\tau G)g$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) $\partial\mathcal L/\partial\dot X=p$ and $\partial\mathcal L/\partial X=S^{\!\top}p$, so $\dot p=S^{\!\top}p$, that is
$\dot\pi=S^\dagger\pi=(-b+\kappa G)\pi$, which is $(D-\kappa G)\pi=0$. Moreover $\pi=m(D+\kappa G)X$, $D(GX)-GDX=[b,G]X$ and $G^2=-\id$.
(ii) Differentiating $g^{\!\top}\eta g=\eta$ gives $(g^{-1}\dot g)^\dagger=-g^{-1}\dot g$, and $(g^{-1}Fg)^\dagger=g^{-1}F^\dagger g$ preserves both summands;
finally $\frac d{d\tau}(g^{-1}Gg)+[b^g,g^{-1}Gg]=g^{-1}[b,G]g$. Checked with fifteen symbolic coordinates for $b$ and a Cayley curve in $SO(3,3)$.
\end{proof}

Part~(ii) is inherited, the transformation law of a connection and of an endomorphism field in any gauge theory. Part~(i) is fixed: the
split-form, internal-connection version of the curved extension at the end of Section~15 of \cite{ES17}, with the single new term
$\kappa(D_\tau G)X$. That the binding part is a tensor restates Theorem~17.3 of \cite{ES17} ($G=G^\dagger$): no gauge transformation creates,
removes or rescales it. The dictionary entry ``$b\leftrightarrow$ transport part of the shift'' is correct, and forced.

\begin{proposition}[Commuting and anticommuting parts]
\label{d4:prop:lp}
$\Ad_G:b\mapsto GbG^{-1}$ is an involution of $\so{3,3}$, so $\so{3,3}=\lG\oplus\mathfrak p_G$, where $\lG=\{[b,G]=0\}$ has dimension $6$ and
$\mathfrak p_G=\{bG=-Gb\}$ has dimension $9$. In the variable $z$,
\[
\lG=\{z\mapsto(P+iQ)z\}\cong\so{3,\C},\qquad \mathfrak p_G=\{z\mapsto(C+i\Sigma)\bar z\},
\]
with $P,Q,C$ real antisymmetric and $\Sigma$ real symmetric. The Killing form $4\Tr$ has signature $(3,3)$ on $\lG$. On $\mathfrak p_G$ it is
negative on the three directions $\Sigma=0$, compact counter-rotations of the two triples such as $R_{12}+R_{45}$, and positive on the
six boosts $C=0$. Both $\mathcal K$ ($z\mapsto-i\bar z$) and the pairing boosts $\sigma_j$ lie in $\mathfrak p_G$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$G^{-1}=-G$ and $G^\dagger=G$ give $(GbG^{-1})^\dagger=-GbG^{-1}$ and $\Ad_G^2=\id$. In $(\bB,\bE)$ blocks, commuting with $G$ means the form
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}P&-Q\\Q&P\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ and anticommuting means $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}C&\Sigma\\\Sigma&-C\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$;
$b^\dagger=-b$ gives the symmetry types. The Killing form was computed in the adjoint representation.
\end{proof}

This is fixed; the description of $\lG$ restates Theorem~12.1 of \cite{ES17}. The pairing boosts that tear the binding at a threshold
(Proposition~16.7 of \cite{ES17}) are anticommuting, and so is the grading root.

\paragraph{Gauge admissibility.} By Theorem~2.3 and Corollary~2.4 of \cite{ES17} (``no gauge boson is a boost''), a Yang--Mills $b$-field with
a positive kinetic term takes values in the compact part $\so3\oplus\so3$ of $\so{3,3}$, spanned by the $R_{ab}$ with $a,b$ in the same
triple. On the two triples,
\begin{equation}
\label{d4:eq:compact}
b=\operatorname{diag}(A+C,\,A-C),\qquad A=[\mathbf a]_\times,\quad C=[\mathbf c]_\times,
\end{equation}
so the triples rotate about $\mathbf a+\mathbf c$ and $\mathbf a-\mathbf c$. The diagonal part $(A,A)$ lies in $\lG$, with $w=\mathbf a$ real
in \eqref{d4:eq:w}; the anti-diagonal part $(C,-C)$ lies in $\mathfrak p_G$, with $\Sigma=0$ ($z\mapsto\mathbf c\times\bar z$). Boost-valued $b$
below ($K_i$, $\sigma_j$, $\mathcal K$, the $\Sigma$-part of $\mathfrak p_G$) are metric connections in the sense of Proposition~16.7 of
\cite{ES17}, legitimate in \eqref{d4:eq:L}, but not gauge fields.

\subsection{The phase-space generator and a criterion for boundedness}
\label{d4:sec:M}

\begin{lemma}[Bookkeeping]
\label{d4:lem:M}
On $(X,\pi)$ the flow is generated by $M=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}-S&\id/m\\0&S^\dagger\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, with $S^\dagger=-b+\kappa G$. It is the
Hamiltonian flow of the Noether energy $H(X,p)=\tfrac1{2m}p^{\!\top}\eta^{-1}p-p^{\!\top}SX$. $H$ vanishes on the invariant Lagrangian subspace $p=0$,
and $\det(\lambda-M)=\chi_S(\lambda)\chi_S(-\lambda)$, where $\chi_S(\lambda)=\det(\lambda-S)$ (since $\dot X=\pi/m-SX$, $\dot\pi=S^\dagger\pi$, and
$S^\dagger$ is similar to $S^{\!\top}$).
\end{lemma}

This is Hamiltonian bookkeeping, inherited. The classification rests on the following criterion.

\begin{theorem}[When the interior motion stays bounded]
\label{d4:thm:criterion}
Let $S$ be any constant real $6\times6$ matrix.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item $M$ is semisimple if and only if $S$ is semisimple and, for every $\mu\in\C$, $\mathcal E_\mu(S^\dagger)$ and $\mathcal E_{-\mu}(S^\dagger)$ are
orthogonal for the bilinear extension of $\eta$.
\item Every solution is bounded on $\R$ if and only if, in addition, $\spec S\subset i\R$. Then (i) says that every eigenspace of $S^\dagger$ is totally
isotropic for $h$. Equivalently, every momentum normal mode $\pi=\Re(ye^{i\omega\tau})$ with $\omega\neq0$, superpositions within a degenerate
frequency included, has period mean $\langle\eta(\pi,\pi)\rangle=\half h(y,y)=0$, and the static momenta ($\omega=0$) span a totally $\eta$-null space.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$M$ is semisimple if and only if $-S$ (its restriction to $\pi=0$) and $S^\dagger$ (the quotient map) are, and $M$ is similar to
$\operatorname{diag}(-S,S^\dagger)$, which by Roth's theorem \cite{Roth52} means that $-S\Xi-\Xi S^\dagger=-\id/m$ is solvable. With $-S=F_1\Lambda_1F_1^{-1}$
and $S^\dagger=F_2\Lambda_2F_2^{-1}$ diagonal, this holds if and only if $(F_1^{-1}F_2)_{ij}=0$ whenever $(\Lambda_1)_i=(\Lambda_2)_j=:\mu$. A row $\ell^{\!\top}$ of
$F_1^{-1}$ has $S^{\!\top}\ell=-\mu\ell$, and $S^{\!\top}=\eta S^\dagger\eta^{-1}$ puts $\eta^{-1}\ell$ in $\mathcal E_{-\mu}(S^\dagger)$; the condition is
$\ell^{\!\top}y=\eta(\eta^{-1}\ell,y)=0$ for $y\in\mathcal E_\mu(S^\dagger)$. (ii): a linear flow is bounded on $\R$ exactly when it is semisimple with
imaginary spectrum, and then $\mathcal E_{-\mu}(S^\dagger)=\overline{\mathcal E_\mu(S^\dagger)}$.
\end{proof}

The theorem is fixed, though valid for any nondegenerate $\eta$ and any $S$. Its necessity half is the split virial identity (Theorem~16.1
of \cite{ES17}: a bound orbit has null momentum on average); the new content is that the averaged virial condition, imposed mode by mode,
is also sufficient. For $S=\kappa G$ it is the isotropy of Proposition~16.3 of \cite{ES17}. On ten test connections, exact semisimplicity,
exact Sylvester solvability over $\Q$ and numerical isotropy (to $10^{-8}$) agree.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Connections commuting with $G$: a Zeeman pattern without a scale}{Connections commuting with G: a Zeeman pattern without a scale}}
\label{d4:sec:lG}

\begin{theorem}[Connections commuting with $G$]
\label{d4:thm:lG}
Let $b\in\lG$ act as in \eqref{d4:eq:w}.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item $\Xi=-G/(2\kappa m)$ solves $-S\Xi-\Xi S^\dagger=-\id/m$, so $M$ is semisimple if and only if $S$ is.
\item $\chi_S(\lambda)=(\lambda^2+\kappa^2)\bigl((\lambda-i\kappa)^2+w\cdot w\bigr)\bigl((\lambda+i\kappa)^2+\overline{w\cdot w}\bigr)$.
\item In the frame in which $b$ is constant, every solution is bounded if and only if $e^{-b\tau}$ is bounded, that is, if and only if $w=0$ or
$w\cdot w>0$. The frequencies are then $\kappa$ and $|\kappa\pm s|$, where $s=\sqrt{w\cdot w}$. For $w\cdot w=0\neq w$ the zero-momentum flow
grows like $\tau^2$; for $w\cdot w\notin[0,\infty)$ it grows exponentially. For a gauge-admissible $b$, $w=\mathbf a$ is real and every
solution is bounded.
\item For the rotation $b=\beta(R_{12}-R_{45})=-\beta L_3$, the spectrum of $M$ is $\pm i\kappa$, $\pm i(\kappa+\beta)$, $\pm i(\kappa-\beta)$, each
double. For the boost $b=\beta K_3$, the exponents are $\pm\beta\pm i\kappa$: complex rather than real, so the orbits spiral outward at the binding
frequency. The growth is the boost factor $e^{-\beta K_3\tau}$ of \cref{d4:prop:larmor}, and $K_3$ is not a gauge generator (Corollary~2.4 of
\cite{ES17}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) With $[b,G]=0$: $-(b+\kappa G)\Xi-\Xi(-b+\kappa G)=\frac1{2\kappa m}(bG-Gb+2\kappa G^2)=-\frac1m$. (ii) On $(V,G)\cong\C^3$, $S$ is
$z\mapsto i\kappa z+w\times z$, and $(w\times)^3=-(w\cdot w)(w\times)$ gives $w\times$ the eigenvalues $0,\pm is$; the real characteristic polynomial
of a complex-linear map is its complex one times the conjugate. (iii) By \cref{d4:prop:larmor}, $X=e^{-b\tau}Y$ with $Y$ a solution at $b=0$,
bounded by (i)--(ii); conversely the zero-momentum solutions are $e^{-b\tau}e^{-\kappa G\tau}X_0$ with $e^{\kappa G\tau}$ bounded. And $e^{-b\tau}$ is
bounded exactly when $w\times$ is semisimple with imaginary eigenvalues: $s$ real, and not $w\cdot w=0\neq w$, where $N=w\times$ has
$N^2\bar w=|w|^2w\neq0=N^3$. For real $w$, $w\times$ is real antisymmetric. (iv) $s=\pm\beta$, respectively $\pm i\beta$; semisimplicity was checked
exactly at $(\kappa,\beta)=(1,\tfrac37)$ and $(1,\tfrac32)$.
\end{proof}

The theorem is fixed, but its content deflates. Criterion~(iii) is a property of the frame in which $b$ is constant, not of the binding: in the
co-moving frame of \cref{d4:prop:larmor} the binding survives every commuting connection, as the paper warns after Proposition~16.7 of
\cite{ES17} (``growth on one worldline proves nothing''), and for admissible $b$ it holds automatically. In that frame a rotation lifts the
degeneracy noted there (``the single frequency is degenerate across the three pairs'') into the triplet $\kappa-\beta$, $\kappa$, $\kappa+\beta$.
Read as a frame vector through the Lie isomorphism of the frame with $\lG$ (Theorem~17.5 of \cite{ES17}), the connection has invariant
$q=w\cdot w$; the interpretive reading is that the lab-frame motion stays bounded exactly for a connection of magnetic type,
$\bE_b\cdot\bB_b=0$ and $|\bB_b|>|\bE_b|$, while the binding survives every commuting connection, and an admissible one has $\bE_b=0$.

\begin{proposition}[The commuting part is a moving frame]
\label{d4:prop:larmor}
Let $b\in\lG$. Then $\bigl(\tfrac d{d\tau}+b+\kappa G\bigr)(e^{-b\tau}Y)=e^{-b\tau}(\dot Y+\kappa GY)$. The exponents are therefore $\pm i\kappa$ shifted by the
eigenvalues of $-b$, and with $p_X=e^{b^{\!\top}\tau}p_Y$,
\[
H_{\rm lab}=H_{\rm co}-p_Y^{\!\top}bY.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$e^{-b\tau}$ is an isometry that commutes with $b$ and $G$; moreover $e^{b\tau}\eta^{-1}e^{b^{\!\top}\tau}=\eta^{-1}$ and
$e^{b\tau}(b+\kappa G)e^{-b\tau}=b+\kappa G$.
\end{proof}

For compact $b$ this is Larmor's theorem \cite{Larmor1897}, inherited (a rotating frame trades the field for an angular velocity), and the
first sentence of Proposition~16.7 of \cite{ES17}; for an $\lG$ boost, $e^{-b\tau}$ is a uniformly boosted frame instead. $Q_b=p_Y^{\!\top}bY$ is
the Noether charge of the $\lG$ generator, so $H_{\rm lab}$ carries a chemical potential for it. The reading is interpretive but sharp: the
Zeeman pattern labels the levels of the binding frequency by their $\lG$ charge, has no gauge-invariant content on one worldline, and is
observable only against a reference that $b$ does not rotate, as the Zeeman effect is against the laboratory. A commuting $b$ supplies a
spectrum but no invariant scale; $\beta/\kappa$ is free.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Connections anticommuting with $G$, and screening}{Connections anticommuting with G, and screening}}
\label{d4:sec:pG}

\begin{theorem}[Connections anticommuting with $G$]
\label{d4:thm:pG}
Let $b\in\mathfrak p_G$ with $b\neq0$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item $(\pm b+\kappa G)^2=b^2-\kappa^2$, and
$M^2=\operatorname{diag}(b^2-\kappa^2,\,b^2-\kappa^2)+N$ with $N=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-2b/m\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\neq0$. Hence $M$ is never
semisimple, and some solutions grow at least linearly.
\item For the admissible counter-rotation $b=\beta(R_{12}+R_{45})$, which is $(C,-C)$ with $\mathbf c=-\beta\mathbf e_3$,
$\det(\lambda-M)=(\lambda^2+\kappa^2)^2(\lambda^2+\kappa^2+\beta^2)^4$. The exponents $\pm i\sqrt{\kappa^2+\beta^2}$ have algebraic multiplicity four and
geometric multiplicity two, and $\pm i\kappa$ are semisimple. The bounded initial data are exactly those with $\pi_1=\pi_2=\pi_4=\pi_5=0$, that is,
with no momentum on the support of $b$. The zero-momentum orbits are bounded and turn at $\sqrt{\kappa^2+\beta^2}$ on the support.
\item For a pairing-plane boost $b=\nu\,\sigma_j$, the exponents in that plane are $\pm\sqrt{\nu^2-\kappa^2}$, and there
$M^2-(\nu^2-\kappa^2)=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-2\nu\sigma_j/m\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$: Jordan blocks for every $\nu\neq0$, below, at and above the
threshold (Proposition~16.7 of \cite{ES17} and the computation after it, where $\nu$ is called $c$).
\item The grading root as a connection satisfies $(\nu\mathcal K+\kappa G)^2=(\nu^2-\kappa^2)\id$ on all of $V$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) $\{b,G\}=0$ gives both squares, and $M^2$ has off-diagonal block $-2b/m$. With $b^2-\kappa^2=Q_s+Q_n$ (Jordan--Chevalley),
$M^2=\operatorname{diag}(Q_s,Q_s)+\bigl(\operatorname{diag}(Q_n,Q_n)+N\bigr)$ with commuting summands ($Q_n$ is a polynomial in $b$), so by uniqueness $M^2$, and
hence $M$, is semisimple only if $\operatorname{diag}(Q_n,Q_n)+N=0$, impossible for off-diagonal $N\neq0$. (ii)--(iv): exact ranks and null spaces over
$\Q(i)$ at $(\kappa,\beta)=(4,3)$ and at $\nu=\tfrac12,1,2$, $\kappa=m=1$ ($\rank(M^2+\tfrac34)=2$ on the plane at $\nu=\tfrac12$), with symbolic
characteristic polynomials; (i) with nine symbolic coordinates on $\mathfrak p_G$.
\end{proof}

Part~(i) is an obstruction: no purely anticommuting connection, admissible or not, is compatible with bounded normal modes. Parts~(ii)
and~(iv) are fixed; (iii) re-verifies Proposition~16.7 of \cite{ES17} and the computation following it. The rate changes to
$\sqrt{\kappa^2+|\mathbf c|^2}$ for an admissible element and $\sqrt{\kappa^2-\nu^2}$ for a pairing boost (a metric connection, not a gauge field), and
the momentum sector behaves as under the positive form of Theorem~15.5 of \cite{ES17}: exponents carried by Jordan blocks, imaginary for a
compact element and for a boost below the threshold.

\begin{proposition}[What a gauge transformation removes]
\label{d4:prop:remove}
Let $b=b_l+b_p\in\lG\oplus\mathfrak p_G$ be constant. Some $g(\tau)\in O(3,3)$ carries $(b,G)$ to $(0,G')$ with $G'$ constant if and only if $[b,G]=0$. For
$b\in\mathfrak p_G$, $e^{b\tau}Ge^{-b\tau}=e^{2b\tau}G$. The gauge-covariant remainder is $D_\tau G=[b,G]=2b_pG$: along a worldline, $b_l$ is pure gauge
and $b_p$ is not.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$b^g=0$ forces $g=e^{-b\tau}g_0$, and then $G'=g_0^{-1}e^{b\tau}Ge^{-b\tau}g_0$ has derivative $g_0^{-1}e^{b\tau}[b,G]e^{-b\tau}g_0$. If $b$ anticommutes
with $G$, then $Gf(b)=f(-b)G$ for every power series $f$.
\end{proof}

This is fixed. A connection on a line has no curvature (the non-abelian $[b_\mu,b_\nu]$ needs two directions; inherited); once the binding root
is present, the invariant is $D_\tau G$, exactly the anticommuting part. One might expect boundedness to force $b\in\lG$. It does not, and the
admissible case shows it.

\begin{theorem}[Compact screening]
\label{d4:thm:screen}
Let $b$ be admissible, as in \eqref{d4:eq:compact}, and $\kappa>0$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item If every solution is bounded, then $\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf c=0$: the two triples rotate at equal rates, $|\mathbf a+\mathbf c|=|\mathbf a-\mathbf c|$.
\item Let $\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf c=0$ and $\mathbf a\neq0$. Then $\spec S=\{\pm i\omega_1,\pm i\omega_2,\pm i\omega_3\}$, where the $\omega_j$ are the roots of
\[
f(\omega)=\omega^3+\kappa\omega^2-(\kappa^2+|\mathbf a|^2+|\mathbf c|^2)\,\omega-\kappa(\kappa^2+|\mathbf c|^2-|\mathbf a|^2),
\]
and if $|\mathbf a|^2\neq\kappa^2+|\mathbf c|^2$, every solution is bounded, although $[b,G]=2b_pG\neq0$ when $\mathbf c\neq0$.
\item If $\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf c=0$, $\mathbf c\neq0$ and $|\mathbf a|^2=\kappa^2+|\mathbf c|^2$, the vector $X_0=\bigl(\mathbf n,(\mathbf a+\mathbf c)\times\mathbf n/\kappa\bigr)$,
$\mathbf n=\mathbf a\times\mathbf c$, is static ($SX_0=0$) with $\eta(X_0,X_0)=-2|\mathbf c|^2|\mathbf n|^2/\kappa^2\neq0$, and some solutions drift linearly.
For $\mathbf a=0$ see \cref{d4:thm:pG}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$S=b+\kappa G$ is real antisymmetric, since $b$ and $G$ are; so $S$ and $S^\dagger=-\eta S\eta=-b+\kappa G$ are normal with imaginary spectrum, and
by \cref{d4:thm:criterion} the motion is bounded if and only if $P_\mu\eta P_\mu=0$ for the orthogonal spectral projectors $P_\mu$ of $S^\dagger$.
(i) Then $\Tr\bigl(\eta(S^\dagger)^2\bigr)=\sum_\mu\mu^2\Tr(P_\mu\eta P_\mu)=0$. But $\{b,G\}$ is block-off-diagonal, $\Tr\eta=0$ and $\Tr[\mathbf v]_\times^2=-2|\mathbf v|^2$,
so $\Tr\bigl(\eta(S^\dagger)^2\bigr)=\Tr(A+C)^2-\Tr(A-C)^2=-8\,\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf c$. (ii) Let $\rho$ be the half-turn about $\mathbf a$ and $\hat\rho=\operatorname{diag}(\rho,\rho)$. Since $\rho\mathbf a=\mathbf a$ and $\rho\mathbf c=-\mathbf c$,
$\hat\rho b\hat\rho=\operatorname{diag}(A-C,A+C)=G^{-1}bG$, so $G':=G\hat\rho$ commutes with $b$ and $G$, hence with $S$ and $S^\dagger$. Moreover $G'^2=-\id$
and $G'^\dagger=G'$, so, as for $G$ in Proposition~16.3 of \cite{ES17}, each eigenspace $\mathcal V_\pm$ of $G'$ (eigenvalue $\pm i$) is totally
$h$-isotropic: $-i\,h(y,y')=h(G'y,y')=h(y,G'y')=i\,h(y,y')$ on $\mathcal V_+$. In the $(\bB,\bE)$ blocks, $\mathcal V_+=\{(\mathbf x,-i\rho\mathbf x)\}$, and $S$ acts
there as $\mathbf x\mapsto(\mathbf a+\mathbf c)\times\mathbf x+i\kappa\rho\mathbf x$; after a common rotation of both triples (which commutes with $G$ and
$\eta$) to $\mathbf a=|\mathbf a|\mathbf e_3$, $\mathbf c=|\mathbf c|\mathbf e_1$, one finds $\det\bigl(i\omega-[\mathbf a+\mathbf c]_\times-i\kappa\rho\bigr)=-i f(\omega)$. So
$S$ has eigenvalues $i\omega_j$ on $\mathcal V_+$ and $-i\omega_j$ on $\mathcal V_-=\overline{\mathcal V_+}$; since $\eta$ exchanges $\mathcal V_\pm$, $S^\dagger$ has the
same eigenvalues there. Every eigenspace of $S^\dagger$ splits along $\mathcal V_\pm$, and lies in one of them unless $f(\omega)=f(-\omega)=0$ for some $\omega$.
But $\half[f(\omega)+f(-\omega)]=\kappa\bigl(\omega^2-\kappa^2-|\mathbf c|^2+|\mathbf a|^2\bigr)$ and $\half[f(\omega)-f(-\omega)]=\omega\bigl(\omega^2-\kappa^2-|\mathbf a|^2-|\mathbf c|^2\bigr)$,
so a common root forces $\mathbf a=0$, or $\omega=0$ and $|\mathbf a|^2=\kappa^2+|\mathbf c|^2$. (iii) With $\mathbf u=\mathbf a+\mathbf c$, $\mathbf v=\mathbf a-\mathbf c$: $SX_0=\bigl(\mathbf u\times\mathbf n-\mathbf u\times\mathbf n,\ \mathbf v\times(\mathbf u\times\mathbf n)/\kappa+\kappa\mathbf n\bigr)=0$,
because $\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf n=0$ and $\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v=|\mathbf a|^2-|\mathbf c|^2=\kappa^2$; and $\eta(X_0,X_0)=|\mathbf n|^2-|\mathbf u|^2|\mathbf n|^2/\kappa^2$. So
$\mathcal E_0(S^\dagger)=\eta\,\mathcal E_0(S)$ is not $\eta$-null, and \cref{d4:thm:criterion} fails at $\mu=0$. Exact checks: the identities symbolically,
boundedness at four rational points (one with $\mathbf a,\mathbf c$ in general position), and the exceptional case at
$(\kappa,|\mathbf c|,|\mathbf a|)=(3,4,5)$ and $(\tfrac35,\tfrac45,1)$.
\end{proof}

The theorem is fixed, and it is the case that matters for a gauge field. Its mechanism is a second binding root $G'=G\hat\rho$ commuting with
$S$. The family was found by the adversarial check (x4-N1: $500$ of $500$ random $\mathbf a\perp\mathbf c$ bounded, none of $500$ generic compact $b$);
part~(i) explains the second count, and the exceptional ratio, of measure zero, was found here. At $\mathbf c=0$ it gives the Zeeman lines of
\cref{d4:thm:lG}. For $\mathbf c\neq0$ the screened frequencies cannot be removed along a worldline (\cref{d4:prop:remove}), but they are $\kappa$ times
functions of $|\mathbf a|/\kappa$ and $|\mathbf c|/\kappa$: screening supplies no scale.

\begin{proposition}[Gyroscopic screening by a boost]
\label{d4:prop:gyro}
Let $b=\alpha L_3+b_\Sigma$, where $b_\Sigma\in\mathfrak p_G$ is the boost $z\mapsto i\Sigma\bar z$ with $\Sigma=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}y_1&y_2&0\\y_2&-y_1&0\\0&0&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$,
and let $r^2=y_1^2+y_2^2>0$. Then $[b,G]\neq0$ and
\[
\chi_S(\lambda)=(\lambda^2+\kappa^2)\bigl(\lambda^2+(\kappa-\alpha)^2-r^2\bigr)\bigl(\lambda^2+(\kappa+\alpha)^2-r^2\bigr).
\]
If $\alpha\neq0$ and $r<\min(|\kappa-\alpha|,|\kappa+\alpha|)$, every solution is bounded; for $\alpha=0$ (\cref{d4:thm:pG}) and for
$|\kappa-\alpha|<r<|\kappa+\alpha|$ some are not. Numerically, near bounded points at which the five conditions of the proof are independent,
the set of $b\in\so{3,3}$ with all motion bounded is a $10$-dimensional submanifold; at the tested points of $\lG$ and of this family the
conditions are degenerate (Jacobian rank four), so these lie on its singular locus.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On $\langle e_1,e_2,e_4,e_5\rangle$, $K_3=GL_3$ is an $\eta$-antisymmetric involution commuting with $G$, $L_3$ and $b_\Sigma$, so its eigenplanes
$\mathcal U_\pm$ are totally $\eta$-null; there $L_3=\mp G$, $S=(\kappa\mp\alpha)G+b_\Sigma$ and $S^\dagger=(\kappa\pm\alpha)G-b_\Sigma$, each squaring to a negative
multiple of $\id$ ($b_\Sigma^2=r^2$, $\{b_\Sigma,G\}=0$). For $\alpha\neq0$ the planes carry different frequencies, so every eigenvector of $S^\dagger$ lies in
$\mathcal U_+$, $\mathcal U_-$ or the $b$-free plane $\langle e_3,e_6\rangle$ and is $h$-isotropic; \cref{d4:thm:criterion} applies (exact checks at
$(\alpha,y_1,y_2)=(\tfrac12,\tfrac14,0)$, $(\tfrac13,\tfrac15,\tfrac17)$, $\kappa=1$). For simple frequencies \cref{d4:thm:criterion}(ii) imposes five real
conditions (two for imaginary spectrum after the trace relation, three for isotropy); their finite-difference Jacobian has rank five at four
least-squares solutions and at an exact compact screened point, and rank four (residual $\sim t^2$ along kernel directions) at three points of
this family and two elliptic points of $\lG$.
\end{proof}

The family is fixed; the dimension statements are numerical. This is gyroscopic stabilisation of a saddle \cite{ThomsonTait1879,Kirillov2013}:
the rotation detunes the two light-like planes of $K_3$ in which the boost acts, so $\eta(\pi,\pi)=2\eta(\pi_+,\pi_-)$ averages to zero. Since
$b_\Sigma$ is a boost, the family is not a gauge field. With \cref{d4:thm:screen} it corrects the working dictionary: an anticommuting part need
not cause secular growth, and boundedness does not single out $\lG$; gauge removability does (\cref{d4:prop:remove}).

\begin{proposition}[A connection alone does not bind]
\label{d4:prop:alone}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item Let $\kappa=0$ and let $b\in\so{3,3}$ be any constant connection, $b=\nu\mathcal K$ included. Then $X=e^{-b\tau}Y$ gives
$\mathcal L=\tfrac m2\eta(\dot Y,\dot Y)$ and $\ddot Y=0$. The generator $M=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}-b&\id/m\\0&-b\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ has
$\det(\lambda-M)=\chi_{-b}(\lambda)^2$ and is never semisimple: the motion is free drift, and there is no binding frequency.
\item The rescaling $(\tau,\kappa,b,m)\mapsto(\vartheta\tau,\kappa/\vartheta,b/\vartheta,\vartheta m)$ maps $M$ to $M/\vartheta$, solutions to solutions and the action
$\int\mathcal L\,d\tau$ to itself, and leaves $\eta$, $G$, $\lG$, $\mathfrak p_G$ and $\so4_\Delta$ unchanged. No value of $\kappa$, hence of $\ell=c/\kappa$, is
selected, with or without a constant $b$.
\item $M(m)=TM(1)T^{-1}$ with $T=\operatorname{diag}(\id,m\id)$: the spectrum of $M$ does not involve $m$, and the rescaling of (ii) keeps $m\kappa$,
not $m/\kappa$, fixed.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) $\dot X+bX=e^{-b\tau}\dot Y$. Write $-\operatorname{diag}(b,b)=S'+N'$ (Jordan--Chevalley). Then $M=S'+(N'+N)$ with
$N=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\id/m\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, and $N'+N\neq0$ because $N$ is off-diagonal and $N'$ is not. (ii) and (iii) are direct.
\end{proof}

Part~(i) is inherited (every connection on a line is pure gauge); its consequence is an obstruction: a Yang--Mills potential on a worldline,
with no self-adjoint shift, produces free motion. Part~(ii) is dimensional analysis, inherited; it restates \cite{ES17} (``$\kappa$ is not
fixed'', end of Section~16; no ratio among the inertia constants and $m$ is fixed by the Clifford structure, Section~17.5). Part~(iii) is fixed
and elementary: nothing in the interior dynamics ties $m$ to $\kappa$, and $mc^2=\hbar\kappa$, that is $\ell=\hbar/(mc)$, is a matching added from
outside, never a result.

\subsection{The split virial identity and the vacuum}
\label{d4:sec:vacuum}

\begin{proposition}[Split virial with a connection]
\label{d4:prop:virial}
For every constant $S$, and in particular for every $b\in\so{3,3}$, $\frac d{d\tau}\eta(X,\pi)=\eta(\pi,\pi)/m$ along every solution.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\eta(\pi/m-SX,\pi)+\eta(X,S^\dagger\pi)=\eta(\pi,\pi)/m$; also checked in integral form along numerical solutions (DOP853, error below $10^{-8}$).
\end{proof}

This restates and re-verifies Theorem~16.1 of \cite{ES17} (fixed there), whose metric-compatible connection is our $b$.

\begin{theorem}[Krein signatures and the zero-point energy]
\label{d4:thm:krein}
Suppose every solution is bounded, and let $\omega>0$ have multiplicity $k_\omega$ in $\spec S$. On the eigenspace $\mathcal E_{i\omega}(M)$, the energy form
$v\mapsto\bar v^{\!\top}\mathcal Hv$, where $\mathcal H$ is the Hessian of $H$, has signature $(k_\omega,k_\omega)$. Hence, with positive-norm
quantisation of the normal modes, the zero-point energy $\sum_j(\pm)\half\hbar\omega_j$ cancels frequency by frequency, and every level has a lower
one.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On $\mathcal E_{i\omega}(M)$, $\mathcal Hv=-i\omega\mathbb Jv$, so the energy form equals $\omega$ times the Krein form $-i\bar v^{\!\top}\mathbb Jv$, which is
nondegenerate there because eigenspaces of distinct imaginary eigenvalues are orthogonal for it \cite{YakubovichStarzhinskii75}. The
zero-momentum part of $\mathcal E_{i\omega}(M)$ has dimension $k_\omega$ (the multiplicity of $i\omega$ in $-S$), and $H$ vanishes on it (\cref{d4:lem:M}).
Since $M$ is semisimple, $\dim\mathcal E_{i\omega}(M)=2k_\omega$. A nondegenerate Hermitian form with a half-dimensional isotropic subspace has signature
$(k,k)$, and a mode of energy sign $\pm$ quantises to $\pm\hbar\omega(n+\half)$.
\end{proof}

The theorem is fixed, though structural (any nondegenerate $\eta$); it was checked numerically (to $10^{-9}$) on the Zeeman triplet, on
$w=(2,i,0)$, on the gyroscopic family, on a compact screened connection and at three constituents. It extends Theorem~18.6 of \cite{ES17}
(zero-point energy $0$ on $\R^{3,3}$ at $b=0$) to every bounded connection: the cancellation, forced by the perfect-square form \eqref{d4:eq:L},
survives the splitting line by line, and so does the absence of a ground state. On the coordinate subfields of that theorem (zero-point energy $\pm\tfrac32\hbar\kappa$ or
$\pm\half\hbar\kappa$), $G$ does not preserve the subfield (the zero-momentum flow stays in it only on its intersection with its preimage under
$G$, of dimension $0$ or $2$), and the theorem does not apply.

\begin{corollary}[No Fock vacuum with Jordan blocks]
\label{d4:cor:nofock}
Suppose $M$ is not semisimple at a frequency $\omega\neq0$, as for every nonzero $b\in\mathfrak p_G$. Then no linear symplectic change of
variables writes the Hamiltonian on that block as a sum of signed oscillators, and there is no Fock vacuum annihilated by normal-mode
lowering operators.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
A sum of signed oscillators generates a semisimple flow, and symplectic changes of variables act by similarity.
\end{proof}

\noindent This is an obstruction. A model block with the same Jordan structure (checked) is $H=\omega(q_1p_2-q_2p_1)+\half(q_1^2+q_2^2)$, whose terms
Poisson-commute \cite{Williamson36}; that its quantisation has continuous spectrum is inherited and not re-derived.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Three constituents: the part of $\mathfrak{ps}_W$ that commutes with $G_3$}{Three constituents: the part of ps\_W that commutes with G3}}
\label{d4:sec:three}

We use the generators $E_{6(k-1)+a}=\omega_6^{\otimes(k-1)}\otimes e_a\otimes\id^{\otimes(3-k)}$, $\omega_6=e_1e_2\cdots e_6$, of equation~(2) of \cite{ES17} on
$\R^{512}$, with the adjoint action of bivectors on the $18$ vectors computed exactly, and set $G_3=G\oplus G\oplus G$. We follow the numbering of
Section~17 of \cite{ES17}: $W=\{7,13,14,15\}$, the colour directions are $\{10,11,12,16,17,18\}$, $E=\operatorname{span}\{7,8,9,13,14,15\}$, and
$\mathrm{rot}(i,j)=e_{i'}e_{j'}-e_ie_j$ acts by $\half\ad$.

\begin{proposition}[The binding-compatible algebra]
\label{d4:prop:PS}
The $E_A$ satisfy the relations of $\Cl(9,9)$, $G_3$ is an anti-isometry with $G_3^2=-\id$, and $\dim(\Cent(G_3)\cap\so{9,9})=72$. For four-dimensional
$U\subset\operatorname{span}\{2,3,7,8,9,13,14,15\}$, neither $\so U$ nor $\so6_C$ meets $\Cent(G_3)$, and their sum $\mathfrak{ps}_U$ meets it in dimension
$\binom d2$, $d=\dim(U\cap E)$; for $U=W$ in $\so4_\Delta=\operatorname{span}\{\mathrm{rot}(i,j):i<j\in W\}$. The elements $T^\pm_1=\half\bigl(\mathrm{rot}(13,14)\pm\mathrm{rot}(7,15)\bigr)$,
$T^\pm_2=\half\bigl(\mathrm{rot}(7,14)\mp\mathrm{rot}(13,15)\bigr)$ and $T^\pm_3=\half\bigl(\mathrm{rot}(7,13)\pm\mathrm{rot}(14,15)\bigr)$ satisfy $[T^+_i,T^-_j]=0$ and
$[T^\pm_1,T^\pm_2]=-T^\pm_3$ cyclically. The trace form is negative definite, so $\so4_\Delta=\su2_+\oplus\su2_-$ is compact. Each $T^\pm_3$ has
eigenvalues $\pm\tfrac i2$, four times each, and $0$, ten times.
\end{proposition}

This restates and re-verifies Theorem~17.21 of \cite{ES17} (fixed there), here for $U=W$, $\{3,7,8,9\}$, $\{2,3,7,8\}$ (dimensions $6,3,1$) and in
\texttt{x4\_check.py} for all $70$ coordinate $U$. Each $\su2$ factor locks a weak rotation of $W$ to the colour rotation of the partners
$\{10,16,17,18\}$; reading $\so4_\Delta$ as ``Yang and Mills' isospin, doubled'' is interpretive, as after Theorem~17.22 of \cite{ES17}.

\begin{proposition}[Spectra on the eighteen-dimensional frame]
\label{d4:prop:so4spec}
Let $\kappa=5$ and $m=1$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item For $b=\beta T^+_3$ with $\beta=2$, $\chi_S=(\lambda^2+25)^5(\lambda^2+16)^2(\lambda^2+36)^2$: frequency $\kappa$ on the five complex singlet directions and
$\kappa\mp\beta/2$ on the two $\su2_+$ doublets, of weights $\pm\half$.
\item For $b=\beta_+T^+_3+\beta_-T^-_3$ with $(\beta_+,\beta_-)=(2,4)$, the lines are $\kappa\pm\half(\beta_+\pm\beta_-)=8,6,4,2$, together with $\kappa$.
\item In both cases $\Xi=-G_3/(2\kappa m)$ solves the Sylvester equation, $M$ is semisimple, the Krein signatures are $(k,k)$, and the zero-point
energy vanishes.
\item The weak-only rotation $R_{7,13}$ and the colour-only rotation $R_{10,16}$ give a non-semisimple $M$, hence secular growth.
\item $b=R_{7,13}\pm R_{10,11}\in\mathfrak{ps}_W$ has $[b,G_3]\neq0$, and every solution is bounded, for every $\kappa>0$. At $\kappa=5$ the frequencies are
$\kappa$ (six pairs) and $|\omega_j|\approx4.319,\,5.050,\,5.731$, with $\omega_j$ the roots of $\omega^3+5\omega^2-26\omega-125$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i)--(iv): exact characteristic polynomials and semisimplicity tests on $36\times36$ integer matrices; Krein signatures numerical. (v) $G_3$ maps
the six-plane $\langle e_7,e_8,e_{13};e_{10},e_{11},e_{16}\rangle$ as $G$ maps $\langle e_1,e_2,e_3;e_4,e_5,e_6\rangle$, and $b$ preserves it and vanishes on its
$\eta$-orthogonal complement, where the motion is the $b=0$ binding. On the six-plane $b$ is admissible, with unit rotation vectors $\mathbf e_2$ and
$\pm\mathbf e_3$ for the two triples: equal rates, perpendicular axes, so $\mathbf a=\half(\mathbf e_2\pm\mathbf e_3)\perp\mathbf c=\half(\mathbf e_2\mp\mathbf e_3)$ and
$|\mathbf a|^2=|\mathbf c|^2=\half\neq\kappa^2+\half$. \Cref{d4:thm:screen}(ii) applies with $f$ as stated; $M$ is also exactly semisimple at $\kappa=5$.
\end{proof}

This is fixed. Every constant $\so4_\Delta$-valued connection is compact and commutes with $G_3$, so by \cref{d4:thm:lG}(i) it keeps the interior
bounded; the splitting follows the $\su2$ weights, $\beta/\kappa$ is free, and by \cref{d4:prop:larmor} the pattern can be removed along one
worldline. The single-factor rotations tear the binding, but boundedness does not single out the locked diagonal: part~(v), found by the
adversarial check (x4-N2) and explained by \cref{d4:thm:screen}, is bounded with $[b,G_3]\neq0$ (the anti-locked $R_{7,13}+R_{10,16}$, $\mathbf a=0$ on its
six-plane, is not). What singles out $\so4_\Delta$ is commutation with $G_3$ (Theorem~17.21 of \cite{ES17}), that is, removability along a worldline
(\cref{d4:prop:remove}).

\subsection{Quantum Yang--Mills facts, and the channels of the paper's vacuum}
\label{d4:sec:qym}

The only place left for $b$ to generate a scale is the quantum theory. We re-verify the facts used by the earlier session.

\begin{proposition}[Inherited facts]
\label{d4:prop:qym}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item For $\su4$ with $\tr(T^AT^B)=\half\delta^{AB}$, $\sum_AT^AZT^A=\half\bigl(\tr(Z)\id-\tfrac14Z\bigr)$ for every $4\times4$ matrix $Z$: $\tfrac{15}8=C_F$ on $\id$,
$-\tfrac18$ on traceless $Z$.
\item The channel factors $T_1\cdot T_2=\half[C_2(\text{pair})-C_2(R_1)-C_2(R_2)]$ are $+\tfrac38,-\tfrac58$ on $\mathbf{10},\mathbf6\subset\mathbf4\otimes\mathbf4$;
$-\tfrac{15}8,+\tfrac18$ on $\mathbf1,\mathbf{15}\subset\bar{\mathbf4}\otimes\mathbf4$; $+\tfrac14,-\tfrac34$ on $\mathbf3,\mathbf1\subset\mathbf2\otimes\mathbf2$ of $\su2$
($C_2=\tfrac{15}8,\tfrac92,\tfrac52,4$ on $\mathbf4,\mathbf{10},\mathbf6,\mathbf{15}$). Hence the Dirac bilinear
$\overline{(\mathbf4,\mathbf2,\mathbf1)}\otimes(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)=(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)\oplus(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ has $\su4$ factors $-\tfrac{15}8,+\tfrac18$, and a
Lorentz-scalar Majorana bilinear of $(\mathbf4,\mathbf1,\mathbf2)$, symmetric in its internal indices, lies in
$\mathrm{Sym}^2(\C^4\otimes\C^2)=(\mathbf{10},\mathbf1,\mathbf3)\oplus(\mathbf6,\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$ ($30+6$), with factors $(+\tfrac38,+\tfrac14)$ and $(-\tfrac58,-\tfrac34)$.
\item $\int_{k^2\le k_{\max}^2}\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\frac1{k^2+M_{\rm dyn}^2}=\frac1{16\pi^2}\bigl[k_{\max}^2-M_{\rm dyn}^2\ln(1+k_{\max}^2/M_{\rm dyn}^2)\bigr]$ (Euclidean);
$f_{\rm gap}(x)=1-x^2\ln(1+x^{-2})$ decreases strictly from $1$ to $0$ ($f_{\rm gap}'<0$ since $\ln(1+u)>u/(1+u)$), so $1=\lambda_{\rm c}f_{\rm gap}(x)$ has a
root $x=M_{\rm dyn}/k_{\max}>0$ if and only if $\lambda_{\rm c}>1$; at $\lambda_{\rm c}=\tfrac74$ it is $x=0.5330234964\ldots$, confirming the earlier $0.533$.
\item The ladder critical coupling $\pi/(3C_F)$ \cite{FukudaKugo76,Miransky93} is $8\pi/45\approx0.5585$ at $C_F=\tfrac{15}8$. For $SU(2)$ with $N_f$
Dirac doublets $\beta_0=(22-2N_f)/3$, $6$ at $N_f=2$ \cite{GrossWilczek73,Politzer73}, and $\Lambda=\mu e^{-8\pi^2/(\beta_0g^2)}$ is independent of $\mu$.
$SU(4)\to Sp(4)$ ($N_f=2$) leaves $15-10=5$ Goldstone directions \cite{Peskin80}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

All of this is inherited \cite{NambuJonaLasinio61,Slansky81}, and was computed exactly (generalised Gell-Mann basis, joint Casimir eigenspaces on
$\mathrm{Sym}^2(\C^8)$), the root of (iii) with mpmath to $25$ digits. The reading of (ii) is interpretive. Under the most-attractive-channel heuristic
of one-boson exchange \cite{RabyDimopoulosSusskind80} (negative factor: attraction), with a common coupling for $\su4$ and $\su2_R$, which the paper
itself adopts only as an assumption (Assumption~19.4 and the remark after Proposition~19.5 of \cite{ES17}), the exchange favours
$(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$ and the colour-breaking $(\mathbf6,\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$, and disfavours $(\mathbf{15},\mathbf2,\mathbf2)$, which the Georgi--Jarlskog factor
$-3$ needs (Proposition~21.2 of \cite{ES17}; \cite{GeorgiJarlskog79}), and $(\mathbf{10},\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$, which carries the paper's Standard-Model vacuum
(Section~20 of \cite{ES17}). That vacuum is a minimum of a scalar potential, not a gauge-exchange condensate, so the heuristic addresses a
different, dynamical scenario, in which a gauge-generated vacuum would point away from the paper's.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Synthesis: where the $b$-field enters}{Synthesis: where the b-field enters}}
\label{d4:sec:synthesis}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \emph{The binding frequency comes from the self-adjoint part, never from a connection} (fixed; obstructed for $b$ alone): $\kappa$, with
$(\kappa G)^2=-c^2/\ell^2$, sits in the tensor part; $b$ alone gives free drift (\cref{d4:thm:covariant,d4:prop:alone}).
\item \emph{A commuting $b$ splits the binding frequency without supplying a scale} (fixed; Zeeman reading interpretive): lines $\kappa$,
$\kappa\pm\sqrt{w\cdot w}$, bounded for admissible $b$, removable along any worldline, $\beta/\kappa$ free (\cref{d4:thm:lG,d4:prop:larmor}); at three
constituents $\so4_\Delta$, the part of the Pati--Salam algebra commuting with the binding root, gives $\su2$-weight lines and is not the only
bounded part (\cref{d4:prop:so4spec}); the zero-point cancellation survives line by line (\cref{d4:thm:krein}).
\item \emph{A purely anticommuting $b$ tears the binding} (obstructed; spectra fixed), with rates $\sqrt{\kappa^2+|\mathbf c|^2}$ (admissible) or
$\sqrt{\kappa^2-\nu^2}$ (pairing boost) carried by Jordan blocks (\cref{d4:thm:pG,d4:cor:nofock}). A mixed one may or may not: an admissible $b$ keeps
every orbit bounded exactly at equal rates of the triples about non-opposite axes, outside $|\mathbf a|^2=\kappa^2+|\mathbf c|^2$
(\cref{d4:thm:screen}); a boost can be screened gyroscopically (\cref{d4:prop:gyro}); neither gives a scale.
\item \emph{The only role left for $b$ in generating a scale is quantum} (open). The arena leaves $\ell$ free (\cref{d4:prop:alone}) and classical
four-dimensional Yang--Mills theory has no dimensionful parameter; a scale arises only by dimensional transmutation,
$\Lambda_b=\mu\,e^{-8\pi^2/(\beta_0g^2)}$ \cite{ColemanWeinberg73,GrossWilczek73}.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{conjecture}[A transmuted binding length]
\label{d4:conj:transmute}
$\ell=\hbar c/\Lambda_b$ for the scale $\Lambda_b$ of a Yang--Mills $b$-field, that is, $\hbar\kappa=\Lambda_b$.
\end{conjecture}

Reading the rest energy $mc^2$ as $\hbar\kappa$ is a separate assumption, not part of the conjecture: the spectrum of $M$ does not involve $m$, and
the rescaling of \cref{d4:prop:alone} keeps $m\kappa$, not $m/\kappa$, fixed. The conjecture is open: nothing in the arena derives it. A mechanism
would need $\kappa$ to be dynamical, as the rotor rate of Section~17 of \cite{ES17} is, and coupled to a quantum gauge sector with a fixed $\beta_0$
and matching coupling; even then, by \cref{d4:prop:qym}(ii), the most attractive channels are not those of the paper's vacuum.

Corrected so that it is true, the user's sentence reads as follows. In $E=mc^2$, read through the added identification $mc^2=\hbar\kappa$, the
symmetric part is the self-adjoint binding shift $\kappa G$, with square $-c^2/\ell^2$. The Yang--Mills $b$-field is the antisymmetric, transport
part of the same shift, and it is compact. Classically it generates neither $m$ nor $\kappa$: when it commutes with $G$ it splits the interior
rate $\kappa$ into charge-labelled lines; a purely anticommuting $b$ tears the binding, and a mixed one may or may not
(\cref{d4:thm:screen,d4:prop:gyro}). Whether its quantum scale is the $\ell$ that the arena leaves free is open.
