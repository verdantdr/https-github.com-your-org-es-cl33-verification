\section{What a mass breaks: duality, chirality, and the two senses of rotation}
\label{d2:sec:breaks}

The hypothesis reads $m$ as what breaks a symmetry of which $c^2$ is the symmetric part. Before any mechanism is proposed, the
prior question has to be answered: which symmetry, exactly, does a mass break in the arena, and which does it leave alone? The
arena carries two different things called mass. In the field-strength reading a frame vector
$X=\sum_i(B_ie_i+E_ie_{i+3})$, $z=\bB+i\bE$, has the energy density $u=\half|z|^2$, the complex invariant $q=z\cdot z$ and the
rest-frame energy density $E_0=\half|q|$ (Section~14 of \cite{ES17}). In the Dirac reading the mass is the kernel $T=e_1e_2e_3=\gamma^0J$
of the Grassmann bilinear (Proposition~4.5 and Corollary~18.4 of \cite{ES17}). This section treats both, in that order, and then
the particle--antiparticle structure, the two vacua and the Goldstone count across the tower. Units are $\hbar=c=1$ except where a
symbol is shown.

The answer in brief. A mass breaks no Lorentz generator and, in the Dirac reading, no generator of $\so{3,3}$ at all. It breaks the
duality rotation generated by the binding root $G$ (in the precise sense of \cref{d2:prop:duality,d2:thm:little}) and the chiral
and Pauli--G\"ursey symmetries of the massless kinetic term, down to $\uu1_J$. The two senses of the Dirac rest rotation are
exchanged by charge conjugation; the two senses of the frame's binding rotation are exchanged by parity. Every finite-dimensional
statement below was checked exactly by the script \texttt{investigations/rest\_energy/verify/d2\_symmetry\_breaking.py}, whose check
labels are collected in \cref{d2:tab:claims}.

\subsection{Duality and the rest energy of a field}
\label{d2:sec:duality}

Write $D_\vartheta=e^{\vartheta G}$. Since $G^2=-\id$, $D_\vartheta=\cos\vartheta\,\id+\sin\vartheta\,G$, and since $G$ acts on $z$ as
multiplication by $i$ (Theorem~12.1 of \cite{ES17}), $D_\vartheta$ is the duality rotation.

\begin{proposition}[Duality on the invariants]
\label{d2:prop:duality}
For every $X$ and $\vartheta$:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] $z(D_\vartheta X)=e^{i\vartheta}z(X)$, $u(D_\vartheta X)=u(X)$ and $q(D_\vartheta X)=e^{2i\vartheta}q(X)$;
\item[(ii)] $D_\vartheta^{\!\top}D_\vartheta=\id$ and $D_\vartheta^{\!\top}\eta\,D_\vartheta=\cos2\vartheta\,\eta+\sin2\vartheta\,\eta G$, so $D_\vartheta$ is an isometry of the
positive form for every $\vartheta$ and of $\eta$ only for $\vartheta\in\pi\Z$;
\item[(iii)] if $q(X)=0$ every $\vartheta$ fixes $q$; if $q(X)\neq0$ exactly $\vartheta\in\pi\Z$ do;
\item[(iv)] if $q\neq0$ there is a Lorentz frame in which $z=(b+ia)\hat e_1$ with $a,b$ real, and then $q=(a^2+b^2)e^{2i\varphi}$ with
$\varphi=\arg(b+ia)$, $\tan\varphi=a/b$: $\arg q$ is twice the Rainich complexion, and $D_{-\varphi}$ carries the field to the single magnetic plane
$\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\,\theta^2\wedge\theta^3$.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) is $G\leftrightarrow i$ and the quadratic character of $q$. For (ii), $G$ is antisymmetric, so $D_\vartheta$ is orthogonal, and
$G^{\!\top}=\eta G\eta$ because $G$ is $\eta$-self-adjoint; expanding $(\cos\vartheta+\sin\vartheta\,\eta G\eta)\eta(\cos\vartheta+\sin\vartheta\,G)$ with
$G^2=-\id$ gives the formula, and $\eta$, $\eta G$ are linearly independent. (iii) follows from
$e^{2i\vartheta}-1=2i\sin\vartheta\,e^{i\vartheta}$. (iv) is Proposition~12.12 of \cite{ES17}: in that frame $F=a\,\theta^0\wedge\theta^1+b\,\theta^2\wedge\theta^3$,
so $z=(b+ia)\hat e_1$ and $(b+ia)^2=(a^2+b^2)e^{2i\varphi}$.
\end{proof}

Items (i), (iii) and (iv) are inherited: they are the classical duality rotation of the Maxwell invariants
\cite{Rainich1925,Calkin65,DeserTeitelboim76}, written in the frame. Item (ii) is fixed by the arena: the generator of duality is the
binding root, which lies in the $\eta$-self-adjoint summand of $\End(V)=\so{3,3}\oplus\{X=X^\dagger\}$ (Theorem~17.3 of \cite{ES17}),
and therefore it is not an infinitesimal isometry of the split form. For $\vartheta\notin\pi\Z$, $D_\vartheta\notin O(3,3)$, so it is the image of no
element of $\mathrm{Pin}(3,3)$ and has no lift to spinors, as Section~17 of \cite{ES17} notes for the rotor; $D_\pi=-\id$ is implemented by $\Gamma$. Duality is a symmetry of the positive form
$u$, and the split-form interior Lagrangian is rotated by it into its $\eta G$ partner, not kept.

\begin{proposition}[The rest energy is the doubly invariant modulus]
\label{d2:prop:rest}
With $\mathbf S=\bE\times\bB$, $u^2-|\mathbf S|^2=\tfrac14|q|^2$, so $u\ge E_0$, with equality exactly when $\mathbf S=0$, that is in the rest frame
of a non-null field. Of the quantities $u$,
$\Re q$, $\Im q$, $E_0$: $u$ is invariant under $U(1)_G$ and not under $\lG$; $\Re q$ and $\Im q$ are invariant under $\lG$ and not under
$U(1)_G$; $E_0=\half|q|$ is invariant under both.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$u^2-|\bE\times\bB|^2=\tfrac14(|\bB|^2+|\bE|^2)^2-|\bE|^2|\bB|^2+(\bE\cdot\bB)^2=\tfrac14(|\bB|^2-|\bE|^2)^2+(\bE\cdot\bB)^2$. The six generators of
$\lG$ commute with $G$ and lie in $\so{3,3}$, so $\eta(X,AX)=\eta(X,GAX)=0$ for each of them; a boost $K_i$ is symmetric for the positive form
and $X^{\!\top}K_iX\neq0$ for generic $X$. The duality statements are \cref{d2:prop:duality}.
\end{proof}

This too is inherited; in units it is $u^2-c^2|\mathbf g|^2=E_0^2$ with momentum density $\mathbf g=\mathbf S/c^2$, the field form of
$E^2-p^2c^2=m^2c^4$. Its content for the hypothesis is the following reading, which is interpretive. The Lorentz orbits of nonzero frame vectors
are labelled by $q\in\C$, since $\lG\cong\so{3,\C}$ acts transitively on every level set of $q$ with the origin removed (proof of Theorem~14.5 of
\cite{ES17}). The duality group acts on the labels by rotation through $2\vartheta$, the origin is its only fixed point, and the stabiliser of a
nonzero label is $\{D_0,D_\pi\}=\{\pm\id\}$. A nonzero rest energy therefore breaks $U(1)_G$ to $\Z_2$ on the space of Lorentz orbits, and
$\arg q$ is the angle along the broken direction. Two qualifications keep the reading honest. The breaking is a property of a configuration, not
of a vacuum: $u\ge E_0$ is minimised by the zero field and no potential selects $|q|\neq0$ (Section~14 of \cite{ES17}). And the symmetry broken
is not a symmetry of the arena's interior Lagrangian, by \cref{d2:prop:duality}(ii).

What the interior dynamics does with duality is sharper. Along $\ddot X=-\kappa^2X$ (Theorem~16.2 of \cite{ES17}) every solution is
$z(\tau)=c_-e^{-i\kappa\tau}+c_+e^{i\kappa\tau}$ with $c_\pm\in\C^3$, and the zero-momentum orbits are $c_+=0$ (Proposition~16.5 of \cite{ES17}).

\begin{proposition}[The interior energy carries duality charge two]
\label{d2:prop:interior}
Along every solution:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] $\mathcal E_\C:=\tfrac m2\bigl(q(\dot z)+\kappa^2q(z)\bigr)=2m\kappa^2\,c_-\cdot c_+$ is conserved, and the Noether energy
$\tfrac m2\eta(\dot X,\dot X)-\tfrac{m\kappa^2}2\eta(GX,GX)$ is $\Re\mathcal E_\C$. It vanishes when either sense is absent: the energy is the
bilinear overlap of the two senses.
\item[(ii)] A constant duality rotation maps solutions to solutions and multiplies $\mathcal E_\C$ by $e^{2i\vartheta}$.
\item[(iii)] The momentum $p=m\eta(\dot X+\kappa GX)$ has $|p|^2=4m^2\kappa^2|c_+|^2$, while
$u(\tau)=\half(|c_-|^2+|c_+|^2)+\Re\bigl(\bar c_-\cdot c_+\,e^{2i\kappa\tau}\bigr)$, which is constant exactly when $\bar c_-\cdot c_+=0$.
\item[(iv)] On a zero-momentum orbit $p=0$, $|p|^2=0$ and the Noether energy is $0$, while $u=\half|c_-|^2$ and $E_0=\half|c_-\cdot c_-|$ are constant
and generically nonzero; the motion is the duality rotation $z=e^{-i\kappa\tau}c_-$, along the direction $\arg q$.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $c_\pm$ in real and imaginary parts and $z$ in $\cos\kappa\tau$, $\sin\kappa\tau$; each identity is then a polynomial identity modulo
$\cos^2+\sin^2=1$, checked exactly. Conceptually: $\dot z+i\kappa z=2i\kappa c_+e^{i\kappa\tau}$ is the image of $\alpha_+X$, which gives the
momentum; $q(\dot z)+\kappa^2q(z)=4\kappa^2c_-\cdot c_+$ because the terms in $e^{\mp2i\kappa\tau}$ cancel; the anti-isometry $\eta(GX,GX)=-\eta(X,X)$
identifies the Noether energy with $\tfrac m2(\Re q(\dot z)+\kappa^2\Re q(z))$; and $q$ has charge two under duality. On $c_+=0$, $\dot X=-\kappa GX$ and
$\tfrac m2\kappa^2(\eta(GX,GX)+\eta(X,X))=0$.
\end{proof}

The proposition is fixed: it is a statement about the interior dynamics of the arena that could have come out otherwise. Item (i) restates
Proposition~16.5(iii) of \cite{ES17}; (ii)--(iv) are added here. Its consequence for the working dictionary is a correction. The duality-invariant
$u=\half|z|^2$ is not a constant of the interior motion, and it is not equivalent to the closure Casimir $|p|^2$: on the zero-momentum orbits, which
are exactly the orbits along which duality acts, $|p|^2=0$ while $u>0$. The two agree only if the field is read off the momentum, $z(\pi)$, as in
the closure variable $w(p)$ of Section~17 of \cite{ES17}. And the conserved energy of the interior is not undivided: it is the real part of a complex
constant of duality charge two, the Lorentz-class partner of $q$, and it is carried by neither sense alone.

\subsection{Little groups: field points and momenta}
\label{d2:sec:little}

On $z$, $\omega\cdot L+\mathbf v\cdot K\in\lG$ acts as $z\mapsto(\omega+i\mathbf v)\times z$, which is $\delta\bE=\mathbf v\times\bB$, $\delta\bB=-\mathbf v\times\bE$
(Theorem~12.3 of \cite{ES17}); so $\lG=\so{3,\C}$ acting by complex cross products, and $G$ acts as $i$.

\begin{theorem}[Stabilisers of field points]
\label{d2:thm:little}
Let $z\neq0$.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] $\Stab_{\lG}(z)=\{(\lambda z)\times\cdot:\lambda\in\C\}$ is abelian of real dimension two. If $q\neq0$, put $a=z/\sqrt q$; then $R=a\times\cdot$
satisfies $R^3=-R$ (a rotation) and $GR$ satisfies $(GR)^3=GR$ (a boost), about the common complex axis $a$: $\so2\oplus\so{1,1}$. If $q=0$, every
element $N$ of the stabiliser has $N^3=0\neq N^2$: null rotations, $\R^2$.
\item[(ii)] In $\lG\oplus\R G$ the stabiliser of a non-null $z$ is the same two-dimensional algebra, with no $G$ component. The stabiliser of a null
$z=\mathbf u+i\mathbf v$ is three-dimensional and isomorphic to $\mathfrak e(2)$: the null rotations together with $L_{\mathbf n}+G$, where
$\mathbf n=\mathbf u\times\mathbf v/|\mathbf u|^2$ is the propagation axis, which acts on the null rotations with eigenvalues $\pm i$. For
$z=(1,i,0)$, $(L_3+G)z=0$ and $e^{\vartheta G}z=e^{-\vartheta L_3}z$.
\item[(iii)] For comparison, in $\lint$ acting on $E=\langle e_1,e_4,e_5,e_6\rangle$, $\Stab(e_1)=\so3$ and $\Stab(e_1+e_6)=\mathfrak e(2)$, both three-dimensional.
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) $a\times z=0$ iff $a\in\C z$. Since $(z\times)^2w=z(z\cdot w)-qw$, $(z\times)^3=-q\,(z\times)$; with $a\cdot a=1$ this is $R^3=-R$, and multiplying by
$G\leftrightarrow i$ gives $(GR)^3=GR$; for $q=0$, $(z\times)^3=0$ while $(z\times)^2w=z(z\cdot w)\neq0$. (ii) The condition is $(a\times z)+i\vartheta z=0$.
Dotting with $z$ gives $i\vartheta q=0$, so $\vartheta=0$ when $q\neq0$. For null $z$, $(\mathbf u\times\mathbf v)\times\mathbf u=|\mathbf u|^2\mathbf v$
and $(\mathbf u\times\mathbf v)\times\mathbf v=-|\mathbf u|^2\mathbf u$ give $\mathbf n\times z=-iz$, hence $(L_{\mathbf n}+G)z=0$; and
$[L_{\mathbf n},z\times\cdot]=(\mathbf n\times z)\times\cdot=-i\,(z\times\cdot)$, a rotation of the plane of null rotations. (iii) is the classical
computation for a timelike and a null four-vector. All three items were also verified exactly on explicit vectors, $z=(1+i,1,i)$ with $q=2i$,
and the null vectors $(1,i,0)$ and $(1+2i,2+i,2-2i)$.
\end{proof}

The theorem is inherited: (iii) is Wigner's classification \cite{Wigner39,WeinbergQFT}, and (i)--(ii) are the classical stabilisers of a bivector and
the identity of helicity and duality on null fields \cite{PenroseRindler84,Calkin65}, which the paper already records on the frame (Example~13.11 of
\cite{ES17}). Its use here is to separate two notions that the hypothesis runs together. The little group of a particle is the stabiliser of its
momentum, three-dimensional whether or not the particle is massive; the stabiliser of a field point is two-dimensional whether or not $q$ vanishes.
In neither case does a mass change the dimension of a Lorentz stabiliser, and in neither case is a Lorentz generator of the theory broken: $q$ and
$P^2$ are invariants. What a nonzero $q$ removes is the duality generator from the stabiliser. A null field point is duality-invariant up to a
rotation about its axis, and Wigner's massless little group $\mathfrak e(2)$ reappears exactly when duality is admitted, its compact generator
being helicity; a non-null field point is not, and duality is broken to $\{\pm\id\}$.

\subsection{The Dirac mass: invariant in six dimensions, breaking the kinetic algebra}
\label{d2:sec:dirac}

With $\gamma^0=e_1$, $\gamma^a=e_{a+3}$, the kinetic kernels are $K^\mu=-\gamma^0\gamma^\mu$ and the mass kernel is $T=\gamma^0J$ (Proposition~4.5 of
\cite{ES17}); a Grassmann bilinear $\psi^{\!\top}M\psi$ is invariant under $\delta\psi=X\psi$ iff $X^{\!\top}M+MX=0$.

\begin{proposition}[The mass form is a $\mathrm{Spin}(3,3)$ invariant; the kinetic form is not]
\label{d2:prop:Tinv}
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] $X^{\!\top}T+TX=0$ for all fifteen bivectors $X=e_ae_b$, and $S^{\!\top}TS=T$ on each one-parameter subgroup they generate. The antisymmetric
$M\in M_8(\R)$ satisfying the fifteen conditions form exactly $\R\,T$.
\item[(ii)] An element $X$ of $\so{3,3}$, acting on $S$ through the bivectors, is a covariance of the kinetic kernels,
$X^{\!\top}K^\mu+K^\mu X=\lambda^\mu{}_\nu K^\nu$ for some real $4\times4$ matrix $\lambda$, iff $X\in\lint\oplus\R J$, of dimension $7$.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) By Lemma~2.1 of \cite{ES17} the trivector $T$ commutes with a bivector sharing two or no indices with $\{1,2,3\}$ and anticommutes with a
mixed one; by Theorem~2.3 the former are the antisymmetric (rotation) bivectors and the latter the symmetric (boost) ones, so $X^{\!\top}T+TX$ is
$[T,X]$ or $\{T,X\}$ accordingly and vanishes. On a subgroup, $S=\cos\theta+\sin\theta X$ commutes with $T$, and $S=\cosh\theta+\sinh\theta X$ satisfies
$S^{\!\top}TS=TS^{-1}S=T$. Uniqueness is Corollary~18.4 of \cite{ES17}; we re-derived it by solving the $960$ linear conditions on the
$28$-dimensional space of antisymmetric matrices, whose solution space is one-dimensional and contains $T$. (ii) Solving the $256$ linear equations in
the fifteen bivector coefficients and the sixteen entries of $\lambda$ gives a seven-dimensional solution space projecting injectively to the
bivectors; $\lint$ and $J$ lie in it by Lorentz covariance and by $[J,\gamma^0\gamma^\mu]=0$.
\end{proof}

Both statements are fixed. Together they say that within $\mathrm{Spin}(3,3)$ the Dirac mass breaks nothing, and that what reduces the
six-dimensional symmetry to the interior one is the kinetic term, the term in which the clock is converted into the spatial triple, and in which
$c$ enters. In the six-dimensional sense the dictionary entry \emph{$c^2$ symmetric, $m$ breaking} is therefore inverted. The breaking it describes
lives elsewhere, in the internal symmetries of the kinetic term.

\begin{proposition}[What the Dirac mass breaks]
\label{d2:prop:massless}
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] The solutions $X\in\End_\R(\R^8)$ of $X^{\!\top}K^\mu+K^\mu X=0$, $\mu=0,\dots,3$, are
$\mathfrak g_{\rm massless}=\operatorname{span}\{\Omega,J,e_2\Omega,e_3\Omega\}=\uu1_\Omega\oplus\su2$, with trace form $-8\,\id$; adding
$X^{\!\top}T+TX=0$ leaves $\R J$.
\item[(ii)] The orbit map $X\mapsto X^{\!\top}T+TX$ on $\mathfrak g_{\rm massless}$ has rank $3$ and image $\operatorname{span}\{T\Omega,Te_2\Omega,Te_3\Omega\}$,
inside the six-dimensional space of $\lint$-invariant kernels. $T\Omega$ commutes with $J$ and is the pseudoscalar mass; $Te_2\Omega$ and $Te_3\Omega$
anticommute with $J$ and are Majorana kernels.
\item[(iii)] With $M=T(m_s+m_p\Omega)$ the field equation $K^\mu\partial_\mu\Psi=M\Psi$ of the Grassmann Lagrangian
$\half\Psi^{\!\top}(K^\mu\partial_\mu-M)\Psi$, at spatial momentum $\partial_a\Psi=Jp_a\Psi$, is $\dot\Psi=-JH\Psi$ with
\begin{equation}
\label{d2:eq:Hmp}
H=\gamma^0\gamma^ap_a+\gamma^0(m_s+m_p\Omega),\qquad H^{\!\top}=H,\qquad [H,J]=0,\qquad H^2=\bigl(|\bp|^2+m_s^2+m_p^2\bigr)\id .
\end{equation}
\item[(iv)] $e^{\alpha\Omega}$ preserves every $K^\mu$ and sends $M\mapsto Me^{2\alpha\Omega}$; the stabiliser in $U(1)_\Omega$ of a nonzero $M$ is $\{\pm\id\}$.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) The $256$ linear conditions on the $64$ entries of $X$ have the four listed solutions and no others (computed exactly); this is Proposition~5.5 of
\cite{ES17}. With $\mu=0$ the condition is $X^{\!\top}=-X$, so mass invariance is $[T,X]=0$, which $J$ satisfies and the other three do not.
(ii) For antisymmetric $X$, $X^{\!\top}T+TX=[T,X]$. By the blade rule $T$ anticommutes with $\Omega$, $e_2\Omega$, $e_3\Omega$ and commutes with $J$, so the
image is spanned by $2T\Omega,2Te_2\Omega,2Te_3\Omega$; $e_2$ and $e_3$ anticommute with $J$, while $T$ and $\Omega$ commute with it. (iii) Varying the
Grassmann Lagrangian gives $K^\mu\partial_\mu\Psi=M\Psi$ because $K^\mu$ is symmetric and $M$ antisymmetric. With $K^0=-\id$ and $J$ commuting with
$\gamma^0\gamma^a$, $\gamma^0$ and $\Omega$, $\dot\Psi=(K^aJp_a-M)\Psi=-(J\gamma^0\gamma^ap_a+J\gamma^0(m_s+m_p\Omega))\Psi=-JH\Psi$. The five matrices
$\gamma^0\gamma^a$ ($a=1,2,3$), $\gamma^0$ and $\gamma^0\Omega=e_4e_5e_6$ are symmetric, square to $\id$ and pairwise anticommute, which gives $H^2$. (iv) $\Omega$ lies in
$\mathfrak g_{\rm massless}$, and $T$ anticommutes with $\Omega$, so $e^{-\alpha\Omega}Te^{\alpha\Omega}=Te^{2\alpha\Omega}$; $e^{2\alpha\Omega}=\id$ iff $\alpha\in\pi\Z$.
\end{proof}

Items (i) and (ii) are fixed: they restate Proposition~5.5 and Theorem~18.5(a) of \cite{ES17}, re-verified, together with the identification of the
three broken directions. Their physical content, that a Dirac mass breaks the axial $U(1)$ and the Pauli--G\"ursey $SU(2)$ of a free massless Dirac
field to fermion number \cite{Pauli57,Gursey58}, is inherited by Theorem~4.3 of \cite{ES17}. Items (iii) and (iv) are inherited as well: they are the
realification of $h=\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\bp+\beta(m_s+i\gamma_5m_p)$ and of the chiral rotation. Read against the frame, the parallel is exact at the
level of identities: in both readings the mass is the modulus of an order parameter of charge two under a $U(1)$ generated by a complex structure
commuting with the relevant Lorentz algebra ($G$ for $\lG$, $\Omega$ for $\lint$), and a nonzero modulus leaves $\{\pm\id\}$. The parallel stops
there. The Dirac mass is a coupling of the Lagrangian, or a value selected by a potential (Theorem~18.5 of \cite{ES17}); the rest energy of a frame
vector is a property of a configuration, and no potential selects it.

\subsection{Particle and antiparticle: the two senses of one rotation}
\label{d2:sec:senses}

At $\bp=0$ the real first-order form $\half\Psi^{\!\top}J\dot\Psi-\half\Psi^{\!\top}H\Psi$ of Section~4 of \cite{ES17} has $H(0)=m\gamma^0=me_1$.

\begin{proposition}[The rest flow]
\label{d2:prop:restflow}
$JH(0)=mT$, so $\Psi(\lambda)=e^{-m\lambda T}\Psi_0=(\cos m\lambda-\sin m\lambda\,T)\Psi_0$. On the eigenspaces $e_1=\pm1$, each four-dimensional and
$J$-invariant, $T=\pm J$, hence $\Psi(\lambda)=e^{\mp m\lambda J}\Psi_0$ and the energy is $\half\Psi^{\!\top}H\Psi=\pm\half m|\Psi|^2$. Moreover
$\gamma^0=-TJ$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$Je_1=e_2e_3e_1=e_1e_2e_3$, and $T=e_1J$ with $[e_1,J]=0$ gives $TP_\pm=\pm JP_\pm$ for $P_\pm=\half(\id\pm e_1)$; $T^2=-\id$ gives the exponential, and
$-TJ=-e_1J^2=e_1$.
\end{proof}

The proposition is inherited, being the realification of $\psi=e^{\mp imt}\psi_0$ on $\beta=\pm1$, with angular frequency $mc^2/\hbar$ in restored units.
What the arena adds is a name for the generator: the rest-frame motion is the one-parameter group of the temporal volume element $T$, the unique
$\mathrm{Spin}(3,3)$-invariant mass form of \cref{d2:prop:Tinv}, and the clock $\gamma^0=-TJ$ is the quotient of that rotation by the charge rotation.
Read in the terms of the hypothesis, which is an interpretive reading and no more, the single rotation $e^{-m\lambda T}$ splits into two senses of
the charge rotation according to the eigenvalue of the clock.

\begin{proposition}[Which operation reverses the sense]
\label{d2:prop:senses}
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] Every unit vector $C$ of the surplus plane $U$ satisfies $C^2=\id$, $[C,T]=0$, $\{C,J\}=0$, $\{C,e_1\}=0$ and $C^{\!\top}H(0)C=-H(0)$. It therefore maps
the solution $e^{-m\lambda J}\Psi_0$ on $e_1=+1$ onto the solution $e^{+m\lambda J}C\Psi_0$ on $e_1=-1$ of the same flow. The parities $P=\gamma^0$,
$\gamma^0J$ and the time reversal $T_{\rm rev}=e_2e_4e_5e_6$ (Theorem~5.3 of \cite{ES17}) keep the sign of the rest energy.
\item[(ii)] On the frame, parity $P$ ($\bE\mapsto-\bE$) acts on $V$ as $\eta$, so $PGP^{-1}=-G$ and $\mathcal L_\kappa(PX)=\mathcal L_{-\kappa}(X)$, with
$\mathcal L_\kappa-\mathcal L_{-\kappa}=-2m\kappa\,\tfrac{d}{d\tau}(\bE\cdot\bB)$; charge conjugation $X\mapsto-X$ and time reversal
$X(\tau)\mapsto-\eta X(-\tau)$ leave $\mathcal L_\kappa=\tfrac m2\eta(\alpha_+X,\alpha_+X)$ invariant. On $z=c_-e^{-i\kappa\tau}+c_+e^{i\kappa\tau}$,
\[
P:(c_-,c_+)\mapsto(\bar c_+,\bar c_-),\qquad C:(c_-,c_+)\mapsto(-c_-,-c_+),\qquad T:(c_-,c_+)\mapsto(-\bar c_-,-\bar c_+).
\]
\item[(iii)] Hence the sense of the Dirac rest rotation is reversed by $C$, $CP$, $CT$ and $CPT$, and the sense of the frame's binding rotation by $P$, $CP$,
$PT$ and $CPT$.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) $C=\cos\phi\,e_2+\sin\phi\,e_3$ squares to $\id$; by the blade rule it anticommutes with $e_1$ and with $J$ and commutes with $T$, so
$C^{\!\top}e_1C=-e_1$ and $Ce^{-m\lambda T}=e^{-m\lambda T}C$. $\gamma^0$ and $\gamma^0J$ commute with $e_1$; $T_{\rm rev}$ commutes with $e_1$, anticommutes
with $J$ and with $T$, so $T_{\rm rev}\Psi(-\lambda)$ solves the same flow on the same eigenspace. (ii) $\eta G\eta=G^{\!\top}=-G$, $\eta$ is an
$\eta$-isometry, and $\mathcal L_\kappa(PX)=\tfrac m2\eta(P(\dot X-\kappa GX),P(\dot X-\kappa GX))$; the total derivative is Theorem~16.6 of \cite{ES17}.
For $T$ the reversal of $\tau$ compensates the sign of $\eta G\eta$. The action on $c_\pm$ follows from $P\colon z\mapsto\bar z$, $C\colon z\mapsto-z$ and
$T\colon z(\tau)\mapsto-\overline{z(-\tau)}$. (iii) Composition multiplies the signs of (i) and (ii); it was checked on the matrices.
\end{proof}

Item (i) is inherited, realised by the arena's $C$ of Theorem~5.1 of \cite{ES17}; items (ii) and (iii) are fixed, (ii) re-verifying and extending
Theorem~16.6 of \cite{ES17}. The contrast is the content. For Dirac matter the two senses are particle and antiparticle: both solve one Lagrangian,
both have angular frequency $m$, each carries an energy of definite sign, and charge conjugation, a symmetry of the free theory, exchanges them. For
the frame the two senses are the kernels of the two factors of $\alpha_-\alpha_+=\partial_\tau^2+\kappa^2$: one carries no momentum, the other all of it;
each alone carries zero energy (\cref{d2:prop:interior}); their frequency $\kappa=c/\ell$ is a conversion rate and not a mass; and they are exchanged
by parity, which maps $\mathcal L_\kappa$ to $\mathcal L_{-\kappa}$ and is a symmetry only up to the total derivative. That the frame's binding is
not a particle--antiparticle pair in the Dirac sense is an interpretive conclusion drawn from these fixed facts. In both readings the reversal is
$CPT$-odd.

\subsection{Two vacua}
\label{d2:sec:vacua}

\begin{proposition}[The Fock vacuum of one momentum]
\label{d2:prop:fock}
Let $\theta_1,\dots,\theta_8$ be $16\times16$ integer matrices, antisymmetric, $\theta_i^2=-\id$, pairwise anticommuting (tensor words in
$\sigma_x,\sigma_z,\varepsilon$), and $\rho(A)=-\tfrac14\sum A_{ij}\theta_i\theta_j$. Then $\rho$ is a Lie homomorphism with
$[\rho(A),\theta_k]=\sum_jA_{jk}\theta_j$; $\chi_{2\rho(J)}=x^6(x^2+4)^4(x^2+16)$ and $\chi_{2\rho(JH)}=x^6(x^2+4E^2)^4(x^2+16E^2)$; the joint
spectrum of $Q=-i\rho(J)$ and $H=-i\rho(JH(\bp))$ is the table of Theorem~5.7 of \cite{ES17}, with a unique charge-zero vacuum at $H=-2E$ and
multiplicities $1,4,6,4,1$ for $H+2E$. At $\bp=0$ the vacuum energy is $-2m$, that is $-2mc^2$ per momentum mode: minus one half of the four rest
energies. The charge conjugation $C=e_2$ lifts to $U=\theta(v_1)\theta(v_2)\theta(v_3)\theta(v_4)/4$, with $v_k$ orthogonal integer vectors,
$|v_k|^2=2$, spanning the $-1$ eigenspace of $C$; $U$ is orthogonal, $U^2=\id$, $UQU^{-1}=-Q$ and $UH(\bp)U^{-1}=H(-\bp)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The $\theta_i$ were found by an exhaustive search over tensor words; their $256$ monomials have rank $256$, so their commutant is $\R\id$ (Theorem~5.6
of \cite{ES17}). The characteristic polynomials were computed exactly at $(\bp,m,E)=(0,1,1)$, $((0,0,3),4,5)$, $((1,2,2),0,3)$ and $((2,3,6),0,7)$, and,
at the first two, the joint eigenspaces as kernels of the real $64\times32$ systems equivalent to $(Q-q)v=(H-h)v=0$. For the lift, a reflection in $v$ is implemented by
$\theta(v)$ up to sign, and the four signs cancel; $U\rho(A)U^{-1}=\rho(CAC^{\!\top})$, with $CJC^{\!\top}=-J$ and $CJH(\bp)C^{\!\top}=JH(-\bp)$ by
Theorem~5.1 of \cite{ES17}.
\end{proof}

This is Theorem~5.7 and Proposition~5.8 of \cite{ES17}, re-verified, and it is inherited: it is the Fock space of four fermionic modes of one Dirac
momentum. The sentence \emph{the vacuum carries the rest energy with the opposite sign} is accurate for it, and only for it.

\begin{proposition}[The frame vacuum]
\label{d2:prop:framevac}
The interior energy is $\mathcal E=\tfrac m2\bigl(\eta(\dot X,\dot X)+\kappa^2\eta(X,X)\bigr)=\sum_a\eta_{aa}\,\tfrac m2(\dot x_a^2+\kappa^2x_a^2)$.
A spatial mode has $p_y=-m\dot y$ and Hamiltonian $-(p_y^2/2m+m\kappa^2y^2/2)$. With canonical, positive-norm quantisation each temporal mode contributes
$\hbar\kappa(n+\half)$ and each spatial mode $-\hbar\kappa(n+\half)$, so on a subspace of signature $(p,q)$ spanned by coordinate directions the
spectrum is $\hbar\kappa\bigl(\sum_Tn_a-\sum_Sn_a\bigr)+\tfrac{\hbar\kappa}2(p-q)$: the zero-point energy is $0$ on $\R^{3,3}$, $+\tfrac32\hbar\kappa$ on
the temporal triple, $-\tfrac32\hbar\kappa$ on the spatial triple, and $\pm\half\hbar\kappa$ on the $(2,1)$ and $(1,2)$ planes.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first identity uses $\eta(GX,GX)=-\eta(X,X)$. The spatial Hamiltonian is the Legendre transform of $-\tfrac m2(\dot y^2-\kappa^2y^2)$. With
$x=\sqrt{\hbar/2m\kappa}\,(a+a^\dagger)$ and $p=i\sqrt{\hbar m\kappa/2}\,(a^\dagger-a)$, $p^2/2m+m\kappa^2x^2/2=\tfrac{\hbar\kappa}2(aa^\dagger+a^\dagger a)$, checked
exactly on truncated ladder matrices below the cut. The cross term of the Lagrangian is a total derivative and changes the momenta by a canonical
transformation, which does not change the spectrum.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No ground state]
\label{d2:cor:noground}
With positive norm the spectrum of the full frame is $\hbar\kappa\Z$, every level is infinitely degenerate, and every state has one of lower energy,
obtained by adding a spatial quantum. Making the spatial energies positive instead requires negative norm (Section~18 of \cite{ES17}).
\end{corollary}

The proposition is fixed, re-verifying Theorem~18.6 of \cite{ES17}: the vanishing of the zero-point energy is tied to $P=Q$, the very condition under
which factorisation and binding coexist (Theorem~16.2 of \cite{ES17}). The corollary is an obstruction under the stated hypothesis of positive norm,
the familiar fate of indefinite oscillators \cite{PaisUhlenbeck50}. The two vacua differ in kind. The Dirac vacuum has energy $-2E$ per momentum,
fermionic and bounded below; the frame's zero-point energy cancels between positive temporal and negative spatial oscillators, and there is no ground
state for it to be the energy of.

\begin{proposition}[Goldstone directions]
\label{d2:prop:goldstone}
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] The $\lint$-invariant kernels supported on $\Gamma=+1$ form a complex line; each $M$ in it anticommutes with $J$, has rank four,
$M^{\!\top}M\propto\half(\id+\Gamma)$ and $\Tr(M^{\!\top}M)^2/(\Tr M^{\!\top}M)^2=\tfrac14$, and its stabiliser in $\mathfrak g_{\rm massless}$ is
$\R(J-J\Gamma)=\R(J+\Omega)$, with a three-dimensional orbit.
\item[(ii)] At three constituents, with $P_*$, $\Phi_0$, $t^R_3$ as in Appendix~A of \cite{ES17}: $[J,\Phi_0]=[J\Gamma,\Phi_0]=2[t^R_3,\Phi_0]\neq0$, and the
tangent spaces at $\Phi_0$ of the orbits of the $21$ Pati--Salam generators and of all $23$ have the same dimension, $9$.
\item[(iii)] For every kernel $K$ that commutes with $J$ and anticommutes with $\Gamma$ (Dirac type), $[J-J\Gamma,K]=-2J\Gamma K\neq0$; for every kernel supported
on $\Gamma=+1$ that anticommutes with $J$, $[J-J\Gamma,K]=0$. At one constituent $J-J\Gamma=J+\Omega$.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) and (ii) are exact computations (at three constituents on $\R^{512}$, with every matrix dyadic and every rank taken over $\Q$); our
reconstruction reproduces the conventions of Theorem~19.2(v) of \cite{ES17}: $\Phi_0$ is antisymmetric, $\{J,\Phi_0\}=0$, $\Phi_0^{\!\top}\Phi_0$ is the
rank-four $\nu_R$ projector of the block and $[t^R_3,\Phi_0]=J\Phi_0$. The identity in (ii) has a one-line reason: $\Phi_0$ lives on $\Gamma=+1$, so
$J\Gamma\Phi_0=J\Phi_0$ and $\Phi_0J\Gamma=\Phi_0J=-J\Phi_0$. (iii) $J$ commutes with $\Gamma$; if $\{K,\Gamma\}=0=[K,J]$ then $KJ\Gamma=-J\Gamma K$, and if $K$
lives on $\Gamma=+1$ and anticommutes with $J$ then $[J,K]=2JK=[J\Gamma,K]$. Finally $J\Gamma=J^2\Omega=-\Omega$. A colour-singlet Dirac-type kernel,
$P_*E_1E_7E_8P_*$ of the bidoublet, was used as an explicit instance.
\end{proof}

Items (i) and (ii) are fixed, re-verifying Theorems~18.5(b) and~22.2 of \cite{ES17}: the would-be Majoron is eaten. Item (iii) is fixed and is the
cross-scale statement: the same element $J-J\Gamma$, of charge $1-\Gamma$, is kept by a Majorana mass on $\Gamma=+1$ and broken by every Dirac-type mass,
at one constituent, where it is $J+\Omega$, and at three. That at the generic electroweak vacuum the broken $1-\Gamma$ direction is not absorbed by the
gauge orbit (Theorem~22.3 of \cite{ES17}) is cited, not re-verified.

\Cref{d2:tab:verdict} collects the answer. The reading \emph{$m$ breaks the symmetry} is true for duality in the frame and for the chiral and
Pauli--G\"ursey symmetries of Dirac matter, and false for every spacetime symmetry, including the six-dimensional one. A mass does not break Lorentz
invariance; it changes which little group a state has, and it removes the duality generator from the stabiliser of a field.

The reading \emph{$c^2$ is the symmetric part} needs more care, and as a dictionary entry it is interpretive. The constant $c$ is a Schur-forced
conversion $C=c\,\id$ between the temporal and the spatial triple (Theorem~3.2 of \cite{ES17}); a constant generates nothing. The operator that carries
it in the interior, $\kappa G$ with $\kappa=c/\ell$, is where $c^2$ appears as $(\kappa G)^2=-c^2/\ell^2$, the squared binding frequency, and $G$ is the
generator of the broken duality. But $G$ is not an infinitesimal isometry of $\eta$ (\cref{d2:prop:duality}): the duality it generates is a symmetry of the
positive form $u$, while the interior Lagrangian is built on $\eta$. These are the duality class and the Lorentz class of Theorem~17.11 of \cite{ES17},
and they exclude each other, so the symmetric part and the Lorentz-invariant dynamics cannot be one object. In the Dirac reading the symmetric part is
the massless kinetic algebra $\uu1_\Omega\oplus\su2$, which the mass cuts to $\uu1_J$; but with respect to $\mathrm{Spin}(3,3)$ the roles are
reversed, the mass form being fully invariant and the kinetic term doing the reducing (\cref{d2:prop:Tinv}). Which term is the symmetric one depends on
which symmetry is meant, and the hypothesis has to say which.

The undivided energy is not the interior's conserved energy. The duality-invariant $u$ is frame-dependent and not conserved by the binding flow, the
interior Noether energy has duality charge two and lives on the overlap of the two senses, and the only quantity of the list invariant under both
Lorentz and duality is $E_0=\half|q|$, the infimum of $u$ over Lorentz frames (\cref{d2:prop:rest,d2:prop:interior}). If the hypothesis wants an undivided energy whose
differentiation is the mass, the arena's candidate is $u$ with its split $u^2=E_0^2+|\mathbf S|^2$; that is the field form of $E^2=p^2c^2+m^2c^4$,
inherited, and the differentiation it describes is the choice of a rest frame, not a breaking.

Finally the $b$-field. At one constituent none of the broken symmetries can carry a Yang--Mills field: duality has no spin lift
(\cref{d2:prop:duality}), $U(1)_\Omega$ is anomalous, and the Pauli--G\"ursey $\su2$ acts on a single doublet and mixes a field with its conjugate
(Proposition~5.5 of \cite{ES17}, \cite{Witten82}). The one gaugeable generator at one constituent is abelian: it is unbroken by the Dirac mass,
since $[T,J]=0$, and it acquires a St\"uckelberg mass only from a vacuum projector that fails to commute with it (Theorem~26.1 of \cite{ES17}), which is not
a Yang--Mills field. In the paper's vacuum analysis the first place where broken non-abelian generators are gauged is three constituents, where the nine
Goldstone directions of $\Phi_0$ are eaten by $Z'$, $W_R^\pm$ and six leptoquarks (\cref{d2:prop:goldstone}, Theorems~21.1 and~22.2 of \cite{ES17}). There the
non-abelian gauge quanta of Yang and Mills \cite{YangMills54} acquire their masses from the breaking, by the mechanism of \cite{EnglertBrout64,Higgs64},
which is inherited, while the fermion masses come from the Yukawa coupling to the same condensate. In the arena, then, the $b$-particle receives mass
from the differentiation; it does not generate it. That is where the $b$-field belongs in the hypothesis, and it is the opposite of the order the
hypothesis proposes.
