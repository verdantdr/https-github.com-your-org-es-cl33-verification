\section{What a mass breaks: duality, chirality, and the two senses of rotation}
\label{d2:sec:breaks}

The hypothesis reads $m$ as what breaks a symmetry of which $c^2$ is the symmetric part. The prior question is which symmetry a mass
actually breaks in the arena, and which it leaves alone. The arena has two things called mass. In the field-strength reading (Theorem~12.3 of
\cite{ES17}) a vector $X=\sum_i(B_ie_i+E_ie_{i+3})$, $z(X)=\bB+i\bE$, has energy density $u(X)=\half|z|^2$ (the $E_6$ of \cref{d1:sec:main}),
complex invariant $q(X)=z\cdot z$ and rest-frame energy density $E_0(X)=\half|q|$ (Section~14 of \cite{ES17}). In the interior these pointwise
functions are evaluated on two fields, the frame field $X(\tau)$ and the momentum field $\pi=\eta^{-1}p$; we always say which. In the Dirac reading the mass is the kernel $T=e_1e_2e_3=\gamma^0J$ of the Grassmann bilinear (Proposition~4.5 and Corollary~18.4 of
\cite{ES17}). In brief: a mass breaks no Lorentz generator and, in the Dirac reading, no generator of $\so{3,3}$; it breaks the duality rotation
generated by the binding root $G$, in the orbit-space sense of \cref{d2:prop:duality,d2:thm:little}, and the chiral and Pauli--G\"ursey symmetries
of the massless kinetic term, down to $\uu1_J$; and the two senses of the Dirac rest rotation are exchanged by charge conjugation, those of the
frame's binding rotation by parity. Units are $\hbar=c=1$ unless a symbol is shown. Every finite-dimensional statement proved here was checked,
exactly or on dyadic-exact float64 data, by \texttt{verify/d2\_symmetry\_breaking.py}, and independently by \texttt{verify/x2\_check.py}; the
checks are listed in \cref{d2:tab:claims}.

\subsection{Duality and the rest energy of a field}
\label{d2:sec:duality}

Write $D_\vartheta=e^{\vartheta G}=\cos\vartheta\,\id+\sin\vartheta\,G$ (since $G^2=-\id$). As $G$ acts on $z$ as multiplication by $i$
(proof of Theorem~12.1 of \cite{ES17}), $D_\vartheta$ is the duality rotation.

\begin{proposition}[Duality on the invariants]
\label{d2:prop:duality}
For every $X$ and $\vartheta$:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] $z(D_\vartheta X)=e^{i\vartheta}z(X)$, $u(D_\vartheta X)=u(X)$, $q(D_\vartheta X)=e^{2i\vartheta}q(X)$;
\item[(ii)] $D_\vartheta^{\!\top}D_\vartheta=\id$ and $D_\vartheta^{\!\top}\eta\,D_\vartheta=\cos2\vartheta\,\eta+\sin2\vartheta\,\eta G$: $D_\vartheta$ preserves the positive form
for every $\vartheta$, and $\eta$ only for $\vartheta\in\pi\Z$;
\item[(iii)] if $q(X)=0$ every $\vartheta$ fixes $q$; if $q(X)\neq0$ exactly $\vartheta\in\pi\Z$ do;
\item[(iv)] if $q\neq0$, there are Lorentz coordinates in which $z=(f_m+if_e)\hat e_1$ with $f_m,f_e$ real; then $q=(f_m^2+f_e^2)e^{2i\varphi}$ with
$\varphi=\arg(f_m+if_e)$, so $\tan\varphi=f_e/f_m$: $\arg q$ is twice the Rainich complexion, and $D_{-\varphi}$ makes the field purely magnetic,
$z=\sqrt{f_m^2+f_e^2}\,\hat e_1$.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) is $G\leftrightarrow i$ and the quadratic character of $q$. (ii) $G$ is antisymmetric, so $D_\vartheta$ is orthogonal; $G$ is
$\eta$-self-adjoint, so $G^{\!\top}=\eta G\eta$, and expanding $(\cos\vartheta+\sin\vartheta\,\eta G\eta)\eta(\cos\vartheta+\sin\vartheta\,G)$ with $G^2=-\id$
gives the formula; $\eta$ and $\eta G$ are linearly independent. (iii) is $e^{2i\vartheta}-1=2i\sin\vartheta\,e^{i\vartheta}$. (iv) Proposition~12.12 of
\cite{ES17} supplies coordinates in which the field is an electric plane of strength $f_e$ orthogonal to a magnetic one of strength $f_m$; then
$e^{-i\varphi}(f_m+if_e)=|f_m+if_e|$. The principal branch of $\arctan(f_e/f_m)$ would give $-|f_m+if_e|$ for $f_m<0$.
\end{proof}

The proposition is inherited: (i) and (iii) are the classical duality rotation of the Maxwell invariants
\cite{Rainich1925,Calkin65,DeserTeitelboim76}, restated from Section~14.3 of \cite{ES17}; (iv) restates Proposition~12.12 of \cite{ES17} with the
branch of $\varphi$ fixed; and (ii), recorded in Section~14.3 and after Theorem~17.23 of \cite{ES17}, is the textbook rotation of
$\half F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$ into $\half F_{\mu\nu}{\star F}^{\mu\nu}$ under the isometry $X\mapsto F_X$ of Theorem~12.3 of \cite{ES17}, which carries $G$
to the Hodge star. What the arena adds (Theorems~12.3, 16.2 and~17.3 of \cite{ES17}) is that the generator of this rotation is the binding root,
which lies in the $\eta$-self-adjoint summand of $\End(V)=\so{3,3}\oplus\{A=A^\dagger\}$. For $\vartheta\notin\pi\Z$, $D_\vartheta\notin O(3,3)$ lifts to no
element of $\mathrm{Pin}(3,3)$ (as noted after Theorem~17.23 of \cite{ES17}). Duality is a symmetry of the positive form $u$; the split-form
interior Lagrangian is rotated by it into its $\eta G$ partner.

\begin{proposition}[The rest energy is the doubly invariant modulus]
\label{d2:prop:rest}
Let $\mathbf S=\bE\times\bB$ be the frame Poynting vector; as $\Btext=-\bB$ (Section~1.4 of \cite{ES17}), $\mathbf S=-\bE\times\Btext=-\mathbf S_{\rm t}$
is minus the textbook one. Then $u^2-|\mathbf S|^2=\tfrac14|q|^2$, so $u\ge E_0$, with equality exactly when $\mathbf S=0$. Of $u$, $\Re q$, $\Im q$ and
$E_0$, the first is invariant under $U(1)_G$ but not under $\lG$, the next two under $\lG$ but not under $U(1)_G$, and $E_0=\half|q|$ under both.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$u^2-|\bE\times\bB|^2=\tfrac14(|\bB|^2+|\bE|^2)^2-|\bE|^2|\bB|^2+(\bE\cdot\bB)^2=\tfrac14(|\bB|^2-|\bE|^2)^2+(\bE\cdot\bB)^2$. The six generators of $\lG$
commute with $G$ and lie in $\so{3,3}$, so $\eta(X,AX)=\eta(X,GAX)=0$; a boost $K_i$ is symmetric for the positive form and $X^{\!\top}K_iX\neq0$ for
generic $X$. The duality statements are \cref{d2:prop:duality}.
\end{proof}

This restates Section~14.3 of \cite{ES17} and is inherited; with units restored it reads $u^2-c^2|\mathbf g|^2=E_0^2$, $\mathbf g=\mathbf S_{\rm t}/c^2$,
the field form of $E^2-p^2c^2=m^2c^4$. Its reading for the hypothesis is interpretive. Lorentz orbits of nonzero vectors are labelled by $q\in\C$,
since $\lG\cong\so{3,\C}$ is transitive on each level set of $q$ minus the origin (proof of Theorem~14.5 of \cite{ES17}); duality rotates the label
through $2\vartheta$, fixes only the origin, and the stabiliser of a nonzero label is $\{D_0,D_\pi\}=\{\pm\id\}$. A nonzero rest energy thus breaks
$U(1)_G$ to $\Z_2$ on the space of Lorentz orbits, and $\arg q$ is the angle along the broken direction. But the breaking is a property of a
configuration, not of a vacuum ($u$ is minimised by the zero field and no potential selects $|q|\neq0$, Section~14.3 of \cite{ES17}), and the
broken symmetry is not one of the interior Lagrangian. What the interior dynamics does with duality is sharper. Every solution of
$\ddot X=-\kappa^2X$ (Theorem~16.2 of \cite{ES17}) is $z(\tau)=c_-e^{-i\kappa\tau}+c_+e^{i\kappa\tau}$, $c_\pm\in\C^3$; the momentum is
$p=m\eta(\dot X+\kappa GX)$, so the momentum field is $\pi=\eta^{-1}p=m\alpha_+X$ with $\alpha_\pm=\partial_\tau\pm\kappa G$ (eq.~(7) of \cite{ES17}).

\begin{proposition}[Frame field and momentum field in the interior]
\label{d2:prop:interior}
Along every solution:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] $\mathcal E_\C:=\tfrac m2\bigl(q(\dot z)+\kappa^2q(z)\bigr)=2m\kappa^2\,c_-\cdot c_+$ is conserved, and the Noether energy
$\tfrac m2\eta(\dot X,\dot X)-\tfrac{m\kappa^2}2\eta(GX,GX)$ is $\Re\mathcal E_\C$; it vanishes when either sense is absent. A constant duality rotation
maps solutions to solutions and multiplies $\mathcal E_\C$ by $e^{2i\vartheta}$.
\item[(ii)] The momentum field is $z(\pi)=2im\kappa\,c_+e^{i\kappa\tau}$, i.e.\ $\pi(\tau)=e^{\kappa\tau G}\pi_0$: it moves by pure duality, and
$u(\pi)=\half|p|^2=2m^2\kappa^2|c_+|^2$, $E_0(\pi)=2m^2\kappa^2|c_+\cdot c_+|$ and $\mathbf S(\pi)$ are constant.
\item[(iii)] The frame field has $u(X)=\half(|c_-|^2+|c_+|^2)+\Re\bigl(\bar c_-\cdot c_+\,e^{2i\kappa\tau}\bigr)$, constant exactly when
$\bar c_-\cdot c_+=0$, and $q(X)=c_-\cdot c_-\,e^{-2i\kappa\tau}+2c_-\cdot c_++c_+\cdot c_+\,e^{2i\kappa\tau}$, so $E_0(X)$ oscillates in general.
\item[(iv)] Both pure senses, $z=e^{-i\kappa\tau}c_-$ and $z=e^{i\kappa\tau}c_+$, are duality rotations of the frame field, with Noether energy $0$ and
constant $u(X)$ and $E_0(X)$. On the first, the zero-momentum orbits, $p=0$ while $u(X)=\half|c_-|^2>0$; on the second $|p|^2=8m^2\kappa^2u(X)$.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\dot z+i\kappa z=2i\kappa c_+e^{i\kappa\tau}$ is $z(\alpha_+X)$, since $G$ acts as $i$; with $|\pi|=|p|$ ($\eta$ is orthogonal) this gives (ii), and
$\overline{z(\pi)}\times z(\pi)$, $\overline{z(\pi)}\cdot z(\pi)$ and $|z(\pi)\cdot z(\pi)|$ are invariant under $z(\pi)\mapsto e^{i\kappa\tau}z(\pi)$. In $q(\dot z)+\kappa^2q(z)$ the terms in $e^{\mp2i\kappa\tau}$
cancel, leaving $4\kappa^2c_-\cdot c_+$; $\eta(GX,GX)=-\eta(X,X)$ identifies the Noether energy with $\tfrac m2\Re(q(\dot z)+\kappa^2q(z))$; $q$ has
duality charge two. (iii) is the expansion of $|z|^2$ and $z\cdot z$, and (iv) is (i)--(iii) at $c_+=0$ or $c_-=0$. Each identity was also checked
exactly as a polynomial identity in the real and imaginary parts of $c_\pm$, modulo $\cos^2\kappa\tau+\sin^2\kappa\tau=1$.
\end{proof}

Items (i) and (ii) restate Proposition~16.5(iii) and~(ii) of \cite{ES17} (fixed there), with the momentum field made explicit; the zero-momentum
half of (iv) restates Proposition~16.5(i). Item (iii) and the other half of (iv) are added and fixed. The proposition corrects the working
dictionary. The quantities that the interior conserves belong to the momentum field: $u(\pi)$, $E_0(\pi)$ and $\mathbf S(\pi)$ are constant already
in the clamped interior, because $\pi$ moves by a duality rotation, and they are the Casimirs of the closure (\cref{d3:thm:frozen}). The same
functions of the frame field are not conserved: $u(X)$ moves unless $\bar c_-\cdot c_+=0$, and $E_0(X)$ oscillates. Nor is $u(X)$ a function of
$|p|^2$: on the zero-momentum orbits $|p|^2=0$ while $u(X)>0$, on the duality rotations of the other sense $|p|^2=8m^2\kappa^2u(X)$. Duality acts
along both pure senses, so duality orbits are not zero-momentum orbits; the zero-momentum sense is singled out by the momentum, not by duality.
And the interior's conserved energy is not undivided: it is the real part of a constant of duality charge two, carried by neither sense alone.

\subsection{Little groups: field points and momenta}
\label{d2:sec:little}

On $z$, $\boldsymbol\omega\cdot L+\bv\cdot K\in\lG$ acts as $z\mapsto(\boldsymbol\omega+i\bv)\times z$, that is $\delta\bB=\boldsymbol\omega\times\bB-\bv\times\bE$,
$\delta\bE=\boldsymbol\omega\times\bE+\bv\times\bB$ (Theorem~12.3 of \cite{ES17}): $\lG=\so{3,\C}$ acting by complex cross products, and $G$ acts as $i$.

\begin{theorem}[Stabilisers of field points]
\label{d2:thm:little}
Let $z\neq0$.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] $\Stab_{\lG}(z)=\{(\zeta z)\times\cdot:\zeta\in\C\}$ is abelian of real dimension two. If $q\neq0$ and $\hat z=z/\sqrt q$, then $R=\hat z\times\cdot$
has $R^3=-R$ (a rotation) and $(GR)^3=GR$ (a boost) about the common complex axis $\hat z$: $\so2\oplus\so{1,1}$. If $q=0$, every element $N$ of the
stabiliser has $N^3=0\neq N^2$: null rotations, $\R^2$.
\item[(ii)] In $\lG\oplus\R G$ a non-null $z$ has the same stabiliser, with no $G$ component. A null $z=\mathbf n_1+i\mathbf n_2$ has a three-dimensional
stabiliser $\cong\mathfrak e(2)$: the null rotations and $L_{\mathbf n}+G$, with $\mathbf n=\mathbf n_1\times\mathbf n_2/|\mathbf n_1|^2=\bB\times\bE/|\bB\times\bE|$ the
propagation axis, acting on the null rotations with eigenvalues $\pm i$. For $z=(1,i,0)$, $(L_3+G)z=0$ and $e^{\vartheta G}z=e^{-\vartheta L_3}z$.
\item[(iii)] In $\lint$ on the interior four-plane $\langle e_1,e_4,e_5,e_6\rangle$, $\Stab(e_1)=\so3$ and $\Stab(e_1+e_6)=\mathfrak e(2)$, both
three-dimensional.
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) $w\times z=0$ iff $w\in\C z$. From $(z\times)^2w=z(z\cdot w)-qw$, $(z\times)^3=-q\,(z\times)$; with $\hat z\cdot\hat z=1$ this is $R^3=-R$, and
$G\leftrightarrow i$ gives $(GR)^3=GR$; for $q=0$, $(z\times)^3=0\neq(z\times)^2$. (ii) The condition is $w\times z+i\vartheta z=0$; dotting with $z$ gives
$i\vartheta q=0$. For null $z$, $\mathbf n_1\cdot\mathbf n_2=0$ and $|\mathbf n_1|=|\mathbf n_2|$ give $\mathbf n\times\mathbf n_1=\mathbf n_2$, $\mathbf n\times\mathbf n_2=-\mathbf n_1$,
so $\mathbf n\times z=-iz$, $(L_{\mathbf n}+G)z=0$, and $[L_{\mathbf n},z\times\cdot]=(\mathbf n\times z)\times\cdot=-i(z\times\cdot)$. (iii) is the classical
computation. All items were also verified exactly at $z=(1+i,1,i)$, $q=2i$, and at the null vectors $(1,i,0)$ and $(1+2i,2+i,2-2i)$.
\end{proof}

The theorem is inherited: (iii) is Wigner's classification \cite{Wigner39,WeinbergQFT}, (i)--(ii) the classical stabilisers of a bivector and the
identity of helicity and duality on null fields \cite{PenroseRindler84,Calkin65}, recorded on the frame in Theorem~13.8 (the axis of a null field)
and Example~13.11 (the $G$-charge is the helicity) of \cite{ES17}. It separates two notions the hypothesis runs together: a particle's little group (of its momentum) is three-dimensional with or without
mass, a field point's stabiliser two-dimensional with or without $q$. A mass changes the type of a Lorentz stabiliser, never its dimension, and
breaks no Lorentz generator, since $q$ and $P^2$ are invariants. What a nonzero $q$ removes is the duality generator: a null field point is
duality-invariant up to a rotation about its axis, so Wigner's $\mathfrak e(2)$ reappears once duality is admitted, with helicity as compact
generator; for a non-null point duality is broken to $\{\pm\id\}$.

\subsection{The Dirac mass: invariant in six dimensions, breaking the kinetic algebra}
\label{d2:sec:dirac}

With $\gamma^0=e_1$, $\gamma^a=e_{a+3}$ the kinetic kernels are $K^\mu=-\gamma^0\gamma^\mu$ and the mass kernel is $T=\gamma^0J$ (Proposition~4.5 of
\cite{ES17}); a Grassmann bilinear $\psi^{\!\top}M\psi$ is invariant under $\delta\psi=Y\psi$ iff $Y^{\!\top}M+MY=0$.

\begin{proposition}[The mass form is a $\mathrm{Spin}(3,3)$ invariant; the kinetic form is not]
\label{d2:prop:Tinv}
On the spinor module $\R^8$:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] Each of the fifteen bivectors $Y=e_ae_b$ satisfies $Y^{\!\top}T+TY=0$, and $(e^{sY})^{\!\top}T\,e^{sY}=T$ for all real $s$. The
antisymmetric $M\in M_8(\R)$ satisfying the fifteen conditions form exactly $\R\,T$.
\item[(ii)] An element $Y\in\so{3,3}$ satisfies $Y^{\!\top}K^\mu+K^\mu Y=\Lambda^\mu{}_\nu K^\nu$ for some real $4\times4$ matrix $\Lambda$ iff
$Y\in\lint\oplus\R J$, of dimension $7$.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) By Lemma~2.1 of \cite{ES17} the trivector $T$ commutes with a bivector sharing two or no indices with $\{1,2,3\}$ and anticommutes with a mixed
one; by Theorem~2.3 the former are the antisymmetric and the latter the symmetric bivectors, so $Y^{\!\top}T+TY$ is $[T,Y]$ or $\{T,Y\}$ and
vanishes. For an antisymmetric $Y$, $e^{sY}=\cos s+\sin s\,Y$ commutes with $T$; for a symmetric one $e^{sY}=\cosh s+\sinh s\,Y$ and
$(e^{sY})^{\!\top}T=Te^{-sY}$. Uniqueness is Corollary~18.4 of \cite{ES17}, re-derived by solving the $960$ linear conditions on the $28$ antisymmetric
entries. (ii) The $256$ linear equations in the fifteen bivector coefficients and the sixteen entries of $\Lambda$ have a seven-dimensional solution
space, projecting injectively to the bivectors; $\lint$ and $J$ lie in it by covariance and by $[J,\gamma^0\gamma^\mu]=0$.
\end{proof}

Item (i) restates Corollary~18.4 of \cite{ES17} (fixed there); item (ii) is added and fixed. Within $\mathrm{Spin}(3,3)$ the Dirac mass breaks nothing,
and what reduces the six-dimensional symmetry to the interior one is the kinetic term, where the clock is converted into the spatial triple and
$c$ enters. In the six-dimensional sense the entry \emph{$c^2$ symmetric, $m$ breaking} is inverted; the breaking it describes lives in the
internal symmetries of the kinetic term.

\begin{proposition}[What the Dirac mass breaks]
\label{d2:prop:massless}
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] The $Y\in\End_\R(\R^8)$ with $Y^{\!\top}K^\mu+K^\mu Y=0$ for $\mu=0,\dots,3$ form the algebra
$\mathfrak g_{\rm massless}=\operatorname{span}\{\Omega,J,e_2\Omega,e_3\Omega\}=\uu1_\Omega\oplus\su2$, with trace form $-8\,\id$; adding
$Y^{\!\top}T+TY=0$ leaves $\R J$.
\item[(ii)] On $\mathfrak g_{\rm massless}$ the orbit map $Y\mapsto Y^{\!\top}T+TY$ has rank $3$ and image $\operatorname{span}\{T\Omega,Te_2\Omega,Te_3\Omega\}$,
inside the six-dimensional space of $\lint$-invariant kernels; $T\Omega$ commutes with $J$ (pseudoscalar mass), $Te_2\Omega$ and $Te_3\Omega$ anticommute
with it (Majorana kernels).
\item[(iii)] For $M=T(m_s+m_p\Omega)$ the field equation $K^\mu\partial_\mu\Psi=M\Psi$ of $\half\Psi^{\!\top}(K^\mu\partial_\mu-M)\Psi$, with
$\partial_a\Psi=Jp_a\Psi$, is $\dot\Psi=-JH\Psi$ where
\begin{equation}
\label{d2:eq:Hmp}
H=\gamma^0\gamma^ap_a+\gamma^0(m_s+m_p\Omega),\qquad H^{\!\top}=H,\qquad [H,J]=0,\qquad H^2=\bigl(|\bp|^2+m_s^2+m_p^2\bigr)\id .
\end{equation}
\item[(iv)] $e^{\xi\Omega}$ preserves each $K^\mu$ and sends $M\mapsto Me^{2\xi\Omega}$; the stabiliser of $M\neq0$ in $U(1)_\Omega$ is $\{\pm\id\}$.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) The $256$ conditions on the $64$ entries of $Y$ have exactly these solutions. At $\mu=0$ the condition is $Y^{\!\top}=-Y$, so mass invariance is
$[T,Y]=0$, which only $J$ satisfies. (ii) For antisymmetric $Y$ the map is $[T,Y]$; by the blade rule $T$ anticommutes with $\Omega$, $e_2\Omega$,
$e_3\Omega$ and commutes with $J$, giving $2T\Omega,2Te_2\Omega,2Te_3\Omega$; $e_2,e_3$ anticommute with $J$, $T$ and $\Omega$ commute with it. (iii) Varying
gives $K^\mu\partial_\mu\Psi=M\Psi$, since $K^\mu$ is symmetric and $M$ antisymmetric. With $K^0=-\id$ and $J$ commuting with $\gamma^0\gamma^a$,
$\gamma^0$, $\Omega$: $\dot\Psi=(K^aJp_a-M)\Psi=-JH\Psi$. The five matrices $\gamma^0\gamma^a$, $\gamma^0$, $\gamma^0\Omega=e_4e_5e_6$ are symmetric, square
to $\id$ and pairwise anticommute, which gives $H^2$. (iv) $\Omega\in\mathfrak g_{\rm massless}$ and $\{T,\Omega\}=0$, so
$e^{-\xi\Omega}Te^{\xi\Omega}=Te^{2\xi\Omega}$.
\end{proof}

Item (i) restates Proposition~5.5 of \cite{ES17}, and the count in (ii) restates Theorem~18.5(a) of \cite{ES17} (both fixed there); the explicit broken
directions of (ii) are added and fixed. Their physical content, that a Dirac mass breaks the axial $U(1)$ and the Pauli--G\"ursey $SU(2)$ of a
massless Dirac field to fermion number \cite{Pauli57,Gursey58}, is inherited by Theorem~4.3 of \cite{ES17}. Items (iii) and (iv) are inherited, the
realification of $h=\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\bp+\beta(m_s+i\gamma_5m_p)$ (Proposition~4.6 of \cite{ES17}) and of the chiral rotation; (iii) was derived
from the Grassmann kernels, and the commuting first-order form $\half\Psi^{\!\top}J\dot\Psi-\half\Psi^{\!\top}H\Psi$ of Section~4 of \cite{ES17} with the
same $H$ has the same flow. In both readings the mass is the modulus of an order parameter of charge two under a $U(1)$ generated by a complex
structure commuting with the Lorentz algebra ($G$ for $\lG$, $\Omega$ for $\lint$), and a nonzero modulus leaves $\{\pm\id\}$; but the Dirac mass is a
coupling, or a value selected by a potential (Theorem~18.5 of \cite{ES17}), while the rest energy of a frame vector is a property of a
configuration that no potential selects.

\subsection{Particle and antiparticle: the two senses of one rotation}
\label{d2:sec:senses}

At $\bp=0$ the real first-order form $\half\Psi^{\!\top}J\dot\Psi-\half\Psi^{\!\top}H\Psi$ of Section~4 of \cite{ES17} has $H(0)=m\gamma^0=me_1$.

\begin{proposition}[The rest flow]
\label{d2:prop:restflow}
$JH(0)=mT$, so $\Psi(\lambda)=e^{-m\lambda T}\Psi_0=(\cos m\lambda-\sin m\lambda\,T)\Psi_0$. On the eigenspaces $e_1=\pm1$, each four-dimensional and
$J$-invariant, $T=\pm J$: $\Psi(\lambda)=e^{\mp m\lambda J}\Psi_0$, with energy $\half\Psi^{\!\top}H\Psi=\pm\half m|\Psi|^2$. Moreover $\gamma^0=-TJ$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$Je_1=e_1e_2e_3$; $T=e_1J$ with $[e_1,J]=0$ gives $T\Pi_\pm=\pm J\Pi_\pm$ for $\Pi_\pm=\half(\id\pm e_1)$; $T^2=-\id$ gives the exponential;
$-TJ=-e_1J^2=e_1$.
\end{proof}

This is inherited, the realification of $\psi=e^{\mp im\lambda}\psi_0$ on $\beta=\pm1$, at angular frequency $mc^2/\hbar$ in restored units. The arena adds a
name: the rest motion is the one-parameter group of the temporal volume element $T$, the unique $\mathrm{Spin}(3,3)$-invariant mass form, and the
clock $\gamma^0=-TJ$ is the quotient of that rotation by the charge rotation. Read interpretively, the single rotation $e^{-m\lambda T}$ splits into
two senses of the charge rotation according to the eigenvalue of the clock. Below $\mathsf C,\mathsf P,\mathsf T$ name the operations; on the frame
they are realised by $C_f=-\id$, $P_f=\eta$ ($\bE\mapsto-\bE$) and $T_f\colon X(\tau)\mapsto-\eta X(-\tau)$, a convention that bundles the reversal of
$\tau$ with the field map $(\bB,\bE)\mapsto(-\bB,\bE)$ of $C_fP_f$.

\begin{proposition}[Which operation reverses the sense]
\label{d2:prop:senses}
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] Every unit vector $C$ of the surplus plane $U$ has $C^2=\id$, $[C,T]=0$, $\{C,J\}=\{C,e_1\}=0$ and $C^{\!\top}H(0)C=-H(0)$; it maps the solution
$e^{-m\lambda J}\Psi_0$ on $e_1=+1$ to the solution $e^{+m\lambda J}C\Psi_0$ on $e_1=-1$ of the same flow, and $C^{\!\top}JC=-J$,
$C^{\!\top}H(\bp)C=-H(-\bp)$. The parities $\gamma^0$, $\gamma^0J$ and the time reversal $T_{\rm rev}=e_2e_4e_5e_6$ (Theorem~5.3 of \cite{ES17}) keep the sign
of the rest energy.
\item[(ii)] On the frame, $P_fGP_f^{-1}=-G$, $\cL_\kappa(P_fX)=\cL_{-\kappa}(X)$ and $\cL_\kappa-\cL_{-\kappa}=-2m\kappa\,\tfrac{d}{d\tau}(\bE\cdot\bB)$, while
$C_f$ and $T_f$ leave $\cL_\kappa=\tfrac m2\eta(\alpha_+X,\alpha_+X)$ invariant. On $z=c_-e^{-i\kappa\tau}+c_+e^{i\kappa\tau}$:
$P_f\colon(c_-,c_+)\mapsto(\bar c_+,\bar c_-)$, $C_f\colon(c_-,c_+)\mapsto(-c_-,-c_+)$, $T_f\colon(c_-,c_+)\mapsto(-\bar c_-,-\bar c_+)$.
\item[(iii)] The sense of the Dirac rest rotation is reversed by $\mathsf C$, $\mathsf{CP}$, $\mathsf{CT}$, $\mathsf{CPT}$; that of the frame's binding
rotation by $\mathsf P$, $\mathsf{CP}$, $\mathsf{PT}$, $\mathsf{CPT}$.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) $C=\cos\sigma\,e_2+\sin\sigma\,e_3$; by the blade rule it anticommutes with $e_1$, $J$ and the $\gamma^a$ and commutes with $T$ and $\Omega$, so
$C^{\!\top}e_1C=-e_1$, $C$ commutes with $e^{-m\lambda T}$, and $CH(\bp)C^{-1}=-H(-\bp)$ (Theorem~5.1 of \cite{ES17}). $\gamma^0$, $\gamma^0J$ commute with
$e_1$; $T_{\rm rev}$ commutes with $e_1$ and anticommutes with $J$ and $T$, so $T_{\rm rev}\Psi(-\lambda)$ solves the same flow on the same eigenspace.
(ii) $\eta G\eta=G^{\!\top}=-G$ and $\eta$ is an $\eta$-isometry, so $\cL_\kappa(P_fX)=\tfrac m2\eta(\dot X-\kappa GX,\dot X-\kappa GX)$; the total derivative
is Theorem~16.6 of \cite{ES17}; for $T_f$ the reversal of $\tau$ compensates the sign. On $z$: $P_f\colon z\mapsto\bar z$, $C_f\colon z\mapsto-z$,
$T_f\colon z(\tau)\mapsto-\overline{z(-\tau)}$. (iii) Signs multiply under composition (also checked on the matrices).
\end{proof}

Item (i) is inherited, realised by the arena's $C$ (Theorems~5.1 and~5.3 of \cite{ES17}); (ii) and (iii) are fixed, (ii) extending Theorem~16.6 of
\cite{ES17}. For Dirac matter the two senses are particle and antiparticle: solutions of one Lagrangian, of angular frequency $m$, each with energy
of definite sign, exchanged by $C$, a symmetry of the Grassmann Lagrangian (it preserves the mass form and every $K^\mu$, Theorem~5.1 of
\cite{ES17}; on the commuting first-order form it reverses the sign of the action). For the frame the two senses are the kernels of the two factors
of $\alpha_-\alpha_+=\partial_\tau^2+\kappa^2$: one carries no momentum, the other all of it; each alone carries zero Noether energy
(\cref{d2:prop:interior}); they are exchanged by parity, a symmetry only up to a total derivative; and their frequency $\kappa=c/\ell$ is a conversion
rate, not a mass. In the interior $m$ and $\kappa$ are independent inputs ($m$ multiplies the Lagrangian, $\kappa$ sets the period), so reading the
frame's $\hbar\kappa$ as $mc^2$ would be an added identification, not a result. That the frame's binding is not a particle--antiparticle pair in
the Dirac sense is an interpretive conclusion from these fixed facts. In both readings the reversal is $\mathsf{CPT}$-odd.

\subsection{Two vacua}
\label{d2:sec:vacua}

\begin{proposition}[The Fock vacuum of one momentum]
\label{d2:prop:fock}
Let $\theta_1,\dots,\theta_8$ be antisymmetric $16\times16$ integer matrices with $\theta_i^2=-\id$, pairwise anticommuting, and
$\rho(A)=-\tfrac14\sum A_{ij}\theta_i\theta_j$, and let $\varepsilon=(|\bp|^2+m^2)^{1/2}$. Then $\chi_{2\rho(J)}(t)=t^6(t^2+4)^4(t^2+16)$, $\chi_{2\rho(JH)}(t)=t^6(t^2+4\varepsilon^2)^4(t^2+16\varepsilon^2)$; the joint
spectrum of $Q=-i\rho(J)$ and $H=-i\rho(JH(\bp))$ is the table of Theorem~5.7 of \cite{ES17}, with a unique charge-zero vacuum at $-2\varepsilon$ and
multiplicities $1,4,6,4,1$ for $H+2\varepsilon$. Charge conjugation $C=e_2$ lifts to $\hat C=\theta(v_1)\cdots\theta(v_4)/4$, with $v_k$ orthogonal
integer vectors, $|v_k|^2=2$, spanning the $-1$ eigenspace of $C$; $\hat C$ is orthogonal, $\hat C^2=\id$, $\hat CQ\hat C^{-1}=-Q$,
$\hat CH(\bp)\hat C^{-1}=H(-\bp)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The $\theta_i$ are tensor words in the $2\times2$ matrices of eq.~(1) of \cite{ES17}, found by search; their $256$ monomials have rank $256$, so their
commutant is $\R\id$ (Theorem~5.6 of \cite{ES17}); $\rho$ is a Lie homomorphism with $[\rho(A),\theta_k]=\sum_jA_{jk}\theta_j$. The characteristic polynomials were computed exactly at four momenta, the joint eigenspaces at
two. $\hat C\rho(A)\hat C^{-1}=\rho(CAC^{\!\top})$, with $CJC^{\!\top}=-J$ and $CJH(\bp)C^{\!\top}=JH(-\bp)$ (Theorem~5.1 of \cite{ES17}).
\end{proof}

This restates Theorem~5.7 and Proposition~5.8 of \cite{ES17} and is inherited: the Fock space of four fermionic modes of one Dirac momentum. Its
energy, $-2m$ per momentum at rest ($-2mc^2$ in restored units), is minus half the rest energies of the four modes; only in that sense does the vacuum
carry the rest energy with the opposite sign, and only this vacuum does.

\begin{proposition}[The frame vacuum]
\label{d2:prop:framevac}
The interior energy is $\mathcal E=\tfrac m2\bigl(\eta(\dot X,\dot X)+\kappa^2\eta(X,X)\bigr)=\sum_a\eta_{aa}\,\tfrac m2(\dot x_a^2+\kappa^2x_a^2)$; a spatial
mode $x$ has $p_x=-m\dot x$ and Hamiltonian $-(p_x^2/2m+m\kappa^2x^2/2)$. With canonical, positive-norm quantisation a temporal mode contributes
$\hbar\kappa(j+\half)$ and a spatial one $-\hbar\kappa(j+\half)$, $j=0,1,\dots$, so on a coordinate subspace with $k$ temporal and $l$ spatial directions the zero-point
energy is $\tfrac{\hbar\kappa}2(k-l)$: $0$ on $\R^{3,3}$, $\pm\tfrac32\hbar\kappa$ on the temporal and spatial triples, $\pm\half\hbar\kappa$ on the
$(2,1)$ and $(1,2)$ planes.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first identity uses $\eta(GX,GX)=-\eta(X,X)$; the spatial Hamiltonian is the Legendre transform of $-\tfrac m2(\dot x^2-\kappa^2x^2)$. With
$x=\sqrt{\hbar/2m\kappa}\,(\hat a+\hat a^\dagger)$, $p_x=i\sqrt{\hbar m\kappa/2}\,(\hat a^\dagger-\hat a)$, $p_x^2/2m+m\kappa^2x^2/2=\tfrac{\hbar\kappa}2(\hat a\hat a^\dagger+\hat a^\dagger\hat a)$,
checked exactly on truncated ladder matrices. The cross term is a total derivative; it shifts the momenta by a canonical transformation and leaves
the spectrum unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No ground state]
\label{d2:cor:noground}
With positive norm the spectrum of the full frame is $\hbar\kappa\Z$, every level is infinitely degenerate, and every state has one of lower energy
(add a spatial quantum). Making the spatial energies positive instead requires negative norm (Section~18 of \cite{ES17}).
\end{corollary}

The proposition restates Theorem~18.6 of \cite{ES17} (fixed there; the per-mode quantisation is the inherited indefinite oscillator): the zero-point
energy vanishes because the signature is neutral, the condition under which factorisation and binding coexist (Theorem~16.2 of \cite{ES17}). The
corollary, which makes explicit for the full space the remark after Theorem~18.6 of \cite{ES17}, is an obstruction under the hypothesis of positive
norm, the familiar fate of indefinite oscillators \cite{PaisUhlenbeck50}. The two vacua differ in kind: the Dirac vacuum has energy $-2\varepsilon$ per
momentum and is bounded below; the frame's zero-point energy cancels between positive temporal and negative spatial oscillators, and there is no
ground state for it to be the energy of.

\Cref{d2:tab:verdict} collects the answer. \emph{$m$ breaks the symmetry} is true for duality in the frame, in the orbit-space sense and for a
configuration rather than a vacuum, and for the chiral and Pauli--G\"ursey symmetries of Dirac matter; it is false for every spacetime symmetry,
the six-dimensional one included: a mass changes which little group a state has and removes the duality generator from the stabiliser of a field.

\emph{$c^2$ is the symmetric part} is an interpretive entry that needs correction. The constant $c$ is the Schur-forced conversion $c\,\id$
between the triples (Theorem~3.2 of \cite{ES17}), and a constant generates nothing. It enters the interior in two places: in $\eta$ itself,
through the conversion that makes $\eta$ dimensionally consistent (Theorem~3.2 and the opening of Section~3 of \cite{ES17}), and in the shift
$\kappa G$, $\kappa=c/\ell$ (eq.~(7) of \cite{ES17}), where $(\kappa G)^2=-c^2/\ell^2$ is definitional ($G^2=-\id$ is adopted in Theorem~16.2 of
\cite{ES17}). Of the two, the conversion inside $\eta$ (Lorentz class) and the shift $\kappa G$, the second generates duality. But $G$ is not an
infinitesimal isometry of $\eta$ (\cref{d2:prop:duality}): duality is a symmetry of the positive form $u$, while the interior Lagrangian is built on
$\eta$. The split form and the positive form lie in the Lorentz and the duality class of Theorem~17.11 of \cite{ES17}, which exclude each other
(remark after that theorem); $c$ sits in both, and the symmetric part and the Lorentz-invariant dynamics cannot be one object. In the Dirac reading the symmetric part is
the massless kinetic algebra $\uu1_\Omega\oplus\su2$, which the mass cuts to $\uu1_J$; for $\mathrm{Spin}(3,3)$ the roles are reversed
(\cref{d2:prop:Tinv}). Which term is symmetric depends on which symmetry is meant, and the hypothesis has to say which.

Nor is the undivided energy the interior's conserved energy. Of the frame field, $u(X)$ depends on the Lorentz frame and is not conserved by the
binding flow, and $E_0(X)$ oscillates; the Noether energy has duality charge two and lives on the overlap of the two senses; the conserved
$u(\pi)$, $E_0(\pi)$ and $\mathbf S(\pi)$ belong to the momentum field (\cref{d2:prop:interior}). Pointwise, the only listed quantity invariant under
both Lorentz transformations and duality is $E_0=\half|q|$, the infimum of $u$ over Lorentz transformations (\cref{d2:prop:rest}). If an undivided
energy whose differentiation is the mass is wanted, the arena's candidate is $u$ of the momentum field, where it is conserved, with its split
$u^2=E_0^2+|\mathbf S|^2$, the inherited field form of $E^2=p^2c^2+m^2c^4$; the differentiation it describes is the choice of a rest frame, not a
breaking.

Finally the $b$-field. An admissible Yang--Mills potential takes compact values (Theorem~2.3 and Corollary~2.4 of \cite{ES17};
\cref{d4:sec:bfield}). The broken generators at one constituent are compact, but none of them can carry a Yang--Mills field: duality is not a
symmetry of the split-form Lagrangian (\cref{d2:prop:duality}) and has no spin lift, $U(1)_\Omega$ is anomalous, and the Pauli--G\"ursey $\su2$ acts
on a single doublet and mixes a field with its conjugate (Proposition~5.5 of \cite{ES17}, \cite{Witten82}). The one gaugeable generator there, $J$,
is abelian and unbroken by the Dirac mass ($[T,J]=0$); in the projector model of Section~26 of \cite{ES17}, where the charge is $J_N/2$, the
normal-plane form of $J$, it acquires a St\"uckelberg mass exactly when the vacuum projector fails to commute with it (Theorem~26.1 and
Corollary~26.2 of \cite{ES17}). In the paper's vacuum analysis broken non-abelian generators are first gauged at three constituents, where the nine
Goldstone directions of $\Phi_0$ are eaten by $Z'$, $W_R^\pm$ and six leptoquarks (\cref{d2:prop:goldstone}; Theorems~21.1 and~22.2 of \cite{ES17}).
There the gauge quanta of Yang and Mills \cite{YangMills54} acquire their masses from the breaking, by the inherited mechanism of
\cite{EnglertBrout64,Higgs64}. The same condensate gives the right-handed neutrino its Majorana mass $|y|r$ (Theorem~21.1(d) of \cite{ES17}, with
the single Yukawa coupling $y$ that Assumption~19.4 of \cite{ES17} adopts), while the charged-fermion and Dirac-neutrino masses require the
bidoublet values of the electroweak stage, which are inputs (Proposition~21.2 of \cite{ES17}); there the $SU(2)_L$ gauge fields, the closest
analogue of the isotopic $b$-field of \cite{YangMills54}, eat three further directions ($W^\pm$, $Z$; Theorem~22.3 of \cite{ES17}). In the arena the
$b$-particle receives mass from the differentiation; it does not generate it. That is where the $b$-field belongs, and it reverses the order the
hypothesis proposes.
