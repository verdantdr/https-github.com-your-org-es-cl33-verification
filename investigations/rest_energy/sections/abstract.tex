We test, inside the split-signature arena $\Cl(3,3)$ of \cite{ES17}, a reading of the rest-energy relation proposed by the author of that
programme: in $E=mc^2$, $c^2$ is the symmetric part, $m$ is what breaks the symmetry, the energy is one undivided six-dimensional entity
whose internal differentiation produces the mass, and the mass is generated by the Yang--Mills $b$-field. The undivided energy exists:
the only rotation- and duality-invariant quadratic energy of the six frame components is the positive form
$E_6=\tfrac12(|\bB|^2+|\bE|^2)$, whose pointwise symmetry group is $U(3)$, and in the closure of the force law it is a Casimir of the momentum
field. It differentiates without any interaction, $E_6^2=E_0^2+|\mathbf S|^2$, with $E_0=\tfrac12|q|$ the complete invariant of Lorentz
transformations and duality and the minimum of $E_6$ over boosts; this is the field-point form of $E^2=(pc)^2+(mc^2)^2$. The constant
$c$ is a conversion forced by Schur's lemma, not a symmetry; the clause ``$c^2$ is the symmetric part'' holds exactly for the
$\eta$-self-adjoint summand $\kappa G$ of the shift, $(\kappa G)^2=-c^2/\ell^2$, which is the only summand that binds. A nonzero rest energy
breaks duality to $\Z_2$ and, for matter, the chiral algebra to $\uu1_J$; it breaks neither Lorentz invariance nor $\mathrm{Spin}(3,3)$,
since the Dirac mass form $e_1e_2e_3$ is invariant under the whole six-dimensional group. The $b$-field is the antisymmetric, connection
summand of the same shift: classically it never generates the binding rate, a part commuting with $G$ only splits the rest frequency into
charge-labelled lines, and at three constituents the compatible algebra is $\su2\oplus\su2$. What remains for it is the one scale the algebra
leaves free, which dimensional transmutation could supply; that identification is a conjecture. We also give the arena's answers on
particles and antiparticles (the two senses of one rotation, exchanged by charge conjugation), the vacuum and its zero-point energy, and
the Goldstone bosons. Every finite-dimensional statement was checked twice, by independent scripts.
