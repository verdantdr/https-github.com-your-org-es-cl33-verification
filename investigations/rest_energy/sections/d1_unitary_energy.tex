\section{The undivided energy and its differentiation}
\label{d1:sec:main}

The hypothesis reads $E=mc^2$ \cite{Einstein1905} with $c^2$ as the symmetric part of the equation and $m$ as what breaks that symmetry; the
agreed clarification asks that energy first be one undivided, six-dimensional entity, and that mass appear through an internal differentiation
of that unity before any interaction is applied. We follow that order: the undivided energy is built from the arena alone and differentiated with
no other field and no coupling, $c$ is restored, and the Yang--Mills field $b$ \cite{YangMills54} enters only in \cref{d1:sec:b}. The points settled in
\cref{s0:sec:question} ($c^2$ alone is no symmetry; a mass breaks no Lorentz invariance; Proposition~27.1, Example~27.3 and Section~14.4 of
\cite{ES17}) are used without repetition. Most of what follows is
inherited electrodynamics or restates results of \cite{ES17}, and is labelled so; the verdicts are in \cref{d1:sec:assess}.

\paragraph{Conventions.} We use the field-strength reading of Theorem~12.3 of \cite{ES17}: $X=\sum_i(B_ie_i+E_ie_{i+3})$, $z=\bB+i\bE\in\C^3$,
the binding root $G$ ($Ge_i=e_{i+3}$, $Ge_{i+3}=-e_i$) acts as $i$, $q(z)=z\cdot z=|\bB|^2-|\bE|^2+2i\,\bE\cdot\bB$, $\eta(X,X)=\Re q$ and
$\eta(X,GX)=-2\,\bE\cdot\bB$. The frame variable is minus the textbook field, $\Btext=-\bB$ (Section~1.4 of \cite{ES17}). With
$h(z,w)=\sum_j\bar z_jw_j$ we put
\begin{equation}
\label{d1:eq:defs}
E_6:=\tfrac12|z|^2=\tfrac12\bigl(|\bB|^2+|\bE|^2\bigr),\qquad E_0:=\tfrac12|q(z)|,\qquad
\mathbf S:=\bE\times\bB=\tfrac i2\,\bar z\times z=-\mathbf S_{\rm t},\quad \mathbf S_{\rm t}:=\bE\times\Btext .
\end{equation}
$\mathbf S$ is the frame Poynting vector of Section~12 of \cite{ES17}, $\mathbf S_{\rm t}$ the textbook one. Applied to the frame field $X$ these are
the frame field's energies; for the momentum field $\pi=\eta^{-1}p$ of the closure we write $E_6(\pi)$, $E_0(\pi)$, $\mathbf S(\pi)$. Units are
Heaviside--Lorentz with $c=1$ until \cref{d1:sec:c}. For cyclic $(i,j,k)$ the diagonal
rotations and boosts of $\lG$ are $L_i=\tfrac12(e_{j+3}e_{k+3}-e_je_k)$ and $K_i=\tfrac12(e_ke_{j+3}-e_je_{k+3})$, acting on vectors by the
commutator: $L_i$ by $\delta\bB=e_i\times\bB$, $\delta\bE=e_i\times\bE$, and $K_i$ by $\delta\bE=e_i\times\bB$, $\delta\bB=-e_i\times\bE$; $\mathfrak p_G$ is
the part of $\so{3,3}$ anticommuting with $G$, so $\so{3,3}=\lG\oplus\mathfrak p_G$. We write the Lorentz-class inertia of
Theorem~17.11 of \cite{ES17} as $N=(\nu_1\eta^{-1}+\nu_2G\eta^{-1})/m$, the paper's $(a,b)$ renamed, so that $b$ denotes only the
Yang--Mills field. These laws, Theorem~12.3, Example~12.4 and Lemma~3.4 of \cite{ES17} ($\Ad_{e_1e_2e_3}|_V=\eta$, $G=-\mathcal K\circ\Ad_{e_1e_2e_3}$) were
checked in the realisation of Section~2 of \cite{ES17} (d1-0); identifiers d1-$n$ refer to the verification script.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{One undivided energy, with pointwise symmetry $U(3)$}{One undivided energy, with pointwise symmetry U(3)}}
\label{d1:sec:unique}

\begin{theorem}[Uniqueness of the undivided energy]
\label{d1:thm:unique}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The symmetric forms on $V$ invariant under the diagonal rotations are spanned by $|\bB|^2$, $|\bE|^2$, $\bB\cdot\bE$, and split as
$\R\,\id\oplus\operatorname{span}\{\eta,\eta G\}$, the first summand commuting and the second anticommuting with $G$.
\item A quadratic energy invariant under rotations and under duality $e^{\vartheta G}$ is a multiple of $E_6$, which is positive definite.
\item The forms invariant under all of $\lG$ are $\operatorname{span}\{\eta,\eta G\}$, that is $|\bB|^2-|\bE|^2$ and $\bE\cdot\bB$; none is definite.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The diagonal rotations act on $V=V^+\oplus V^-$ as $\R^2\otimes\R^3$ with $SO(3)$ on the second factor, which is absolutely irreducible, so by
Schur's lemma \cite{Schur} the invariant forms are $N=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\gamma\\ \gamma&\beta\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\otimes\id$.
With $G=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\otimes\id$, $\tfrac12(N-GNG)=\tfrac12(\alpha+\beta)\id$ and
$\tfrac12(N+GNG)=\tfrac12(\alpha-\beta)\eta-\gamma\,\eta G$. Since $G^{\!\top}G=\id$ and $G^{\!\top}=-G$, $N$ is $e^{\vartheta G}$-invariant iff
$G^{\!\top}N+NG=0$ iff $[N,G]=0$, which gives (2). For (3) the boost law gives
$\delta(\alpha|\bB|^2+\beta|\bE|^2+2\gamma\,\bB\cdot\bE)=-2(\alpha+\beta)\,\bv\cdot(\bE\times\bB)$, the $\gamma$ term being unchanged, so $\beta=-\alpha$.
\end{proof}

This restates Theorem~17.11(iii) and Proposition~17.4 of \cite{ES17} on $V$, and is inherited: for any binding root $G'=bGb^{-1}$, $b\in SO(3,3)$,
the forms invariant under the compact part of $\mathfrak l_{G'}$ and under $G'$ are the multiples of the positive $b^{-\top}b^{-1}$, so the statement
could not have failed; it is the uniqueness of the energy density among rotation- and duality-invariant forms. The arena adds only that this form
is the Euclidean one of its basis: $G$ is $\eta$-self-adjoint and anticommutes with $\Ad_{e_1e_2e_3}|_V=\eta$, so $G^{\!\top}G=\eta G\eta G=\id$
(Lemma~3.4 of \cite{ES17}; d1-1a, with an exact conjugated root). $E_6$ has duality weight $0$, while $e^{\vartheta G}$ turns $(\Re q,\Im q)$ through
$2\vartheta$: $E_6$ is undivided, distinguishing neither the magnetic triple from the electric one nor, as we now show, rest from motion.

\begin{proposition}[The pointwise symmetry of the undivided energy is $U(3)$]
\label{d1:prop:u3}
(1) The maps of $V$ preserving $E_6$ and commuting with $G$ form $\uu3$, of dimension $9$: $A=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}P&Q\\-Q&P\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$
acts on $z$ by the anti-Hermitian matrix $P-iQ$. (2) The Lie algebra of $O(3,\C)\times U(1)_G$, the $G$-linear maps preserving $q$ up to a phase, is
$\lG\oplus\R G$, of dimension $7$. (3) Their intersection is $\so3\oplus\R G$, of dimension $4$, the maximal compact subalgebra of $\lG\oplus\R G$; and
$\uu3\cap\lG=\so3$. (4) The stabiliser of the function $E_0$ in $U(3)$ is $U(1)\cdot O(3)=\{e^{i\varphi}O\}$; for instance the unitary map
$(1,0,0)\mapsto\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,i,0)$ carries a field at rest ($E_0=E_6=\tfrac12$) to a null field ($E_0=0$) at the same $E_6$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(1) Commuting with $G$ forces the block form, which is multiplication by $P-iQ$ on $z$; $A^{\!\top}=-A$ means $P^{\!\top}=-P$, $Q^{\!\top}=Q$, so $P-iQ$
is anti-Hermitian; $3+6=9$. (2) $q(e^{tM}z)=e^{i\varphi t}q(z)$ gives $M^{\!\top}+M=i\varphi\,\id$, so $M=\tfrac{i\varphi}2+M_0$ with $M_0\in\so{3,\C}=\lG$
(Theorem~12.1 of \cite{ES17}), and $i\,\id$ is $G$. (3) A complex antisymmetric anti-Hermitian matrix is real. (4) If $U\in U(3)$ preserves $|q|$, the
form $z^{\!\top}U^{\!\top}Uz$ vanishes on the cone $q=0$; $q$ is irreducible, so $U^{\!\top}U=e^{i\varphi}\id$, and $O=e^{-i\varphi/2}U$ is unitary and complex
orthogonal, hence real.
\end{proof}

Parts (1)--(3) are inherited Hermitian linear algebra, which makes ``unitary'' literally true of the pointwise form. Part (4) is inherited as well,
the stabiliser of $|z\cdot z|$ among unitary maps of the Riemann--Silberstein vector; read as a statement about the rest energy, it is the exact
sense in which $E_0$ ``breaks'' something: $U(3)$ is reduced to $U(1)\cdot O(3)$, and the five-dimensional orbit $U(3)/U(1)\cdot O(3)$ parametrises
the real structures $z\mapsto\bar z$ up to phase, of which the arena has chosen one (\cref{d1:prop:Tparity}; d1-1b, d1-8b). But $U(3)$ is the symmetry
of the pointwise form $E_6$ only. It is not a symmetry of Maxwell's equations on the frame, $\partial_tz=-i\curl z$ (Proposition~12.10 of \cite{ES17}):
the unitary of (4) carries the wave $(1,i,0)e^{ik(x_3-t)}$ of Example~12.11 to $\tfrac{1+i}{\sqrt2}(1,1,0)e^{ik(x_3-t)}$, which does not solve them. Nor
is it one of the split-form interior, since $G=i\,\id\in U(3)$ reverses $\eta$ and $U(3)\cap O(\eta)=O(3)$; of the closures it is a symmetry only for
$N=\id/m$, the $U(3)$-invariant forms being the multiples of $\id$.

\subsection{The rest energy is the complete invariant of Lorentz and duality}
\label{d1:sec:orbits}

\begin{theorem}[Orbits]
\label{d1:thm:orbits}
(1) Each level set $\{|q|=r^2\}$, $r>0$, is one orbit of $O(3,\C)\times U(1)_G$, of real dimension $5$, with two-dimensional stabilisers; every
non-null $z$ is carried to $(r,0,0)$, $r=\sqrt{2E_0}$. (2) The nonzero null fields form one orbit of $SO(3,\C)$ alone, of dimension $4$, and at a null
point $Gz$ is tangent to the Lorentz orbit. (3) Hence $E_0$ is a complete invariant of Lorentz and duality on non-null fields, and $E_6$ is the
complete invariant of $U(3)$, whose orbits are the spheres.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(1) Write $q(z)=r^2e^{i\varphi}$ and $w=e^{-i\varphi/2}z/r$ (a duality rotation), so $q(w)=1$. Since $q$ is nondegenerate on $w^\perp=\{f:f\cdot w=0\}$,
some $f\in w^\perp$ has $f\cdot f=1$; with $f_3=w\times f$, $(a\times b)\cdot(a\times b)=(a\cdot a)(b\cdot b)-(a\cdot b)^2$ gives $f_3\cdot f_3=1$, so
$A=(w\,f\,f_3)^{\!\top}\in SO(3,\C)$ and $Ae^{-i\varphi/2}z=re_1$. An element $M_0+\tfrac{i\varphi}2$ fixes $e_1$ iff $\varphi=0$ and $M_0$ is a complex
rotation about $e_1$: the stabiliser is $\so{2,\C}$ and the orbit has dimension $7-2=5$. (2) A rotation carries a null $z$ to $\lambda(1,i,0)$, $\lambda>0$; $K_3$ acts by $\delta z=i\,e_3\times z$ and $i\,e_3\times(1,i,0)=(1,i,0)$, so the
Doppler boost $e^{\chi K_3}$, $\chi=-\log\lambda$, finishes. The Lorentz tangent space at a null $z$ is its $q$-orthogonal complement, which contains
$iz$; at $(1,i,0)$, $iz=-e_3\times z$ is the rotation $L_3$, the helicity of Example~13.11 of \cite{ES17}. (3) follows.
\end{proof}

Inherited: the classification of a field strength at a point \cite{Synge56,LandauLifshitz2} (d1-2a, d1-2b). Without duality the complete
invariant is $q$, whose phase (twice the complexion of Proposition~12.12 of \cite{ES17}) duality removes.

\subsection{The differentiation identity}
\label{d1:sec:identity}

\begin{proposition}[Differentiation]
\label{d1:prop:lagrange}
For every $z\in\C^3$
\begin{equation}
\label{d1:eq:split}
|z|^4=|z\cdot z|^2+|\bar z\times z|^2,\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad E_6^2=E_0^2+|\mathbf S|^2 .
\end{equation}
The rank-one Hermitian $H=zz^\dagger$ carries all three terms: $\tr H=2E_6$, $(\Im H)_{jk}=\epsilon_{jkl}S_l$, $\tr(H\bar H)=|q|^2=4E_0^2$, $H^2=(\tr H)H$.
Hence $E_0\le E_6$, with equality iff $\mathbf S=0$, iff $\bar z\parallel z$, iff $\bB\parallel\bE$ or one of them vanishes.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\bar z\times z=-2i\,\bE\times\bB$, and $(a\times b)\cdot(c\times d)=(a\cdot c)(b\cdot d)-(a\cdot d)(b\cdot c)$ at $(\bar z,z,z,\bar z)$ gives
$|\bar z\times z|^2=|z|^4-|q|^2$. Next $H_{jk}=B_jB_k+E_jE_k+i\epsilon_{jkl}(\bE\times\bB)_l$ and $\tr(H\bar H)=|\sum_jz_j^2|^2$. The equality case is
Cauchy--Schwarz for $|h(\bar z,z)|\le|z|^2$.
\end{proof}

Inherited, a complex Lagrange identity; the relation $u^2-|\mathbf S|^2=\tfrac14|q|^2$ is stated in Section~14.3 of \cite{ES17} (d1-3a). In the user's
terms it is exact: the undivided $E_6$ is the hypotenuse of a right triangle whose legs are a Lorentz-invariant rest part $E_0$ and a motion part
$|\mathbf S|$, and the split needs no interaction and no second field; in Minkowski terms $E_0$ is the Lorentz length of $(E_6,\mathbf S)$, although
$(E_6,\mathbf S_{\rm t})$ is not a four-vector (\cref{d1:prop:rainich}).

\begin{proposition}[Rainich identity]
\label{d1:prop:rainich}
For the stress--energy tensor of $(\bE,\Btext)$, $T^{00}=\tfrac12(|\bE|^2+|\Btext|^2)=E_6$, $T^{0i}=(\mathbf S_{\rm t})_i$,
$T^{ij}=T^{00}\delta_{ij}-E_iE_j-B_{{\rm t}i}B_{{\rm t}j}$, one has $T^\mu{}_\mu=0$ and
\begin{equation}
\label{d1:eq:rainich}
T^\mu{}_\lambda T^\lambda{}_\nu=E_0^2\,\delta^\mu{}_\nu ,\qquad T_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}=4E_0^2 .
\end{equation}
$T^{\mu\nu}$ is duality invariant, and for every unit timelike observer $t$ the density $P(t)^\mu=T^\mu{}_\nu t^\nu$ is future causal with
$\eta(P(t),P(t))=E_0^2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Duality invariance is a polynomial identity; both sides of \eqref{d1:eq:rainich} are covariant, so by \cref{d1:thm:orbits} it suffices to take
$z=(r,0,0)$, where $T^\mu{}_\nu=\tfrac{r^2}2\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1,-1)$, and null fields follow by continuity. Causality of $P(t)$ is the dominant
energy condition \cite{HawkingEllis73}.
\end{proof}

Inherited \cite{Rainich1925,Synge56} (d1-3b). The mass of a field point is the Lorentz scalar of the tensor whose $00$ component is the undivided
energy, but $(u,\mathbf S_{\rm t})$ is not a four-vector: each observer sees a different density $P(t)$, all of length $E_0$.

\subsection{The rest energy as a minimum, and two boost factors}
\label{d1:sec:min}

\begin{theorem}[Variational form of the rest energy]
\label{d1:thm:min}
\begin{enumerate}
\item For non-null $z$, $E_0$ is the minimum of $E_6$ over the Lorentz orbit, and over the Lorentz--duality orbit, attained exactly where
$\mathbf S=0$; for null $z\neq0$ the infimum $0$ is not attained. Moreover $E_6=E_0/\sqrt{1-v_E^2}$ with $v_E:=|\mathbf S|/E_6<1$.
\item If $\bB\parallel\bE\parallel\mathbf n$ and the boost $e^{\chi K}$ of rapidity $\chi$ has axis orthogonal to $\mathbf n$, then $E_6=E_0\cosh2\chi$,
$|\mathbf S|=E_0\sinh2\chi$ and $v_E=\tanh2\chi=2\beta/(1+\beta^2)$, $\beta=\tanh\chi$; a boost along $\mathbf n$ changes neither $E_6$ nor $\mathbf S=0$.
\item The observer moving with $\mathbf v_{\rm rest}=\mathbf S_{\rm t}/(E_6+E_0)$ sees $\mathbf S=0$ and $E_6=E_0$; $|\mathbf v_{\rm rest}|=\beta$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(1) By \eqref{d1:eq:split} $E_6\ge E_0$ with equality iff $\mathbf S=0$, and \cref{d1:thm:orbits} supplies points with $\mathbf S=0$ in both orbits; for
null $z$ the Doppler boost drives $E_6$ to $0$. Divide \eqref{d1:eq:split} by $E_6^2$. (2) Take $\mathbf n=e_1$, $X=\sigma_1e_1+\sigma_2e_4$. By Example~12.4 of
\cite{ES17}, $[K_3,e_1]=e_5$, $[K_3,e_5]=e_1$, $[K_3,e_4]=-e_2$, $[K_3,e_2]=-e_4$, so $\ad_{K_3}^3=\ad_{K_3}$,
$e^{\chi\ad_{K_3}}=\id+\sinh\chi\,\ad_{K_3}+(\cosh\chi-1)\ad_{K_3}^2$, $\bB=(\sigma_1\cosh\chi,-\sigma_2\sinh\chi,0)$, $\bE=(\sigma_2\cosh\chi,\sigma_1\sinh\chi,0)$,
and $\mathbf S=-E_0\sinh2\chi\,e_3$; likewise for $K_2$, while $K_1$ fixes $e_1$ and $e_4$. (3) $|\mathbf S|/(E_6+E_0)=\sinh2\chi/(\cosh2\chi+1)=\tanh\chi$. For the direction: $e^{\chi K_3}$ is the passage to a frame moving with
$-\tanh\chi\,e_3$ (Section~12 of \cite{ES17}), in which the rest frame moves along $+e_3$, and so does $\mathbf S_{\rm t}=-\mathbf S$.
\end{proof}

Inherited \cite{LandauLifshitz2}; verified with the paper's own $K_i$, symbolically and numerically; the boost built from $\bE\times\bB$ instead
of $\mathbf S_{\rm t}$ leaves a Poynting vector of at least $0.2\,E_0$ (d1-4a--d1-4c). This is the rest-frame velocity $\mathbf S/(u+\tfrac12|q|)$, with $\tfrac12|q|=u\sqrt{1-v_E^2}$, of Section~14.4 of
\cite{ES17}, where $\mathbf S$ is the textbook $\mathbf S_{\rm t}$, as Section~1.4 of \cite{ES17} stipulates for formulas from the literature; with the
frame vector $\mathbf S=\bE\times\bB$, which Section~12 also calls the Poynting vector, the direction would be reversed. A field point carries two boost
factors, $E_6=E_0\cosh2\chi$ against $E=mc^2\cosh\chi$ for a particle, because the energy density is a component of a second-order tensor and is
quadratic in the boost (the standard behaviour of $T^{00}$): the rest frame moves with $\beta$, the energy with $\beta\oplus\beta$, and an observer
riding with the energy velocity sees the same $E_6$ and the reversed Poynting vector. $E_0$ is a rest-energy density of a field point, not the rest
energy of a particle carried by the flux.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Restoring $c$: where $c^2$ enters}{Restoring c: where c squared enters}}
\label{d1:sec:c}

Adding the dimensionless $G$ to a derivative along a clock coordinate needs a length $\ell$ (Section~3 of \cite{ES17}):
\begin{equation}
\label{d1:eq:kappa}
\kappa=c/\ell,\qquad \alpha_\pm=\partial_\tau\pm\kappa G .
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[$c^2$ is an isotropic weight]
\label{d1:prop:onec}
Let the temporal triple carry $\bB$ and the spatial triple $\bE$, $C:V^+\to V^-$ the conversion and
$G_C=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-C^{-1}\\ C&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, $G_C^2=-\id$. (1) $G_C$ commutes with the diagonal rotations iff $C=c\,\id$.
(2) For $C=c\,\id$ the forms invariant under the diagonal rotations and under $e^{\vartheta G_c}$ are the multiples of $c^2|\bB|^2+|\bE|^2$.
(3) $(\kappa G)^2=-c^2/\ell^2$, and $\ell$ is not fixed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(1) The lower-left block of $[G_C,R\oplus R]$ is $CR-RC$, and Schur's lemma gives $C=c\,\id$: Theorem~3.2 of \cite{ES17}. (2) For
$N=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\gamma\\ \gamma&\beta\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\otimes\id$,
$G_c^{\!\top}N+NG_c=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}2c\gamma&c\beta-\alpha/c\\ c\beta-\alpha/c&-2\gamma/c\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\otimes\id$, zero iff
$\gamma=0$ and $\alpha=c^2\beta$. (3) $G^2=-\id$.
\end{proof}

Part (1) is Theorem~3.2 of \cite{ES17}, fixed there; part (2) is inherited; verified exactly, including the failure for
$C=\operatorname{diag}(c_1,c_2,c_3)$ with distinct entries (d1-5b). Even the weight is a unit convention: $\Phi=\operatorname{diag}(c,c,c,1,1,1)$
conjugates $G_c$ to $G$ and turns $c^2|\bB|^2+|\bE|^2$ into $|\Phi X|^2$, so with $\bB$ measured as $c\bB$ (Heaviside--Lorentz units) $c$ disappears from
$E_6$ and appears only in $\rho_0=u_0/c^2$ below.

\begin{proposition}[SI form]
\label{d1:prop:SI}
With $\varepsilon_0\mu_0c^2=1$, $z_c:=c\bB+i\bE$, $u=\tfrac{\varepsilon_0}2|z_c|^2$, $\mathbf g=\mathbf S_{\rm t}/c^2$ with $\mathbf S_{\rm t}=\bE\times\Btext/\mu_0$,
$q_c:=z_c\cdot z_c=c^2|\Btext|^2-|\bE|^2-2ic\,\Btext\cdot\bE$ and $u_0:=\tfrac{\varepsilon_0}2|q_c|$, \cref{d1:prop:lagrange} applied to $z_c$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{d1:eq:SI}
u^2=c^2|\mathbf g|^2+u_0^2,\qquad \rho_0:=u_0/c^2,\qquad [u]=[c\,\mathbf g]=[u_0]=\mathrm{J\,m^{-3}},\quad [\rho_0]=\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}} .
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

Inherited (d1-5a); calling $\rho_0$ a rest-mass density is a reading, since $(u,c\,\mathbf g)$ is not a four-vector. This is the exact content of
``$c^2$ is the symmetric part'': $c$ is the unique conversion between the triples that commutes with their common rotations, the residual symmetry of
both $E_6$ and $E_0$; in SI units it enters $E_6$ as the isotropic weight $c^2$ of $|\bB|^2$, which a choice of units removes. It is a unit conversion,
not a symmetry, and nothing breaks it: $u_0$ uses the same $c$, and $\rho_0=u_0/c^2$ only converts units.

\subsection{Finite configurations: the mass of a whole}
\label{d1:sec:whole}

\begin{proposition}[Mass of a whole]
\label{d1:prop:whole}
\begin{enumerate}
\item For a free field with compactly supported Cauchy data, $(U,\mathbf P)=\int(u,\mathbf S_{\rm t})\,d^3x$ is a four-vector and
$M^2:=U^2-|\mathbf P|^2\ge U\int(u-|\mathbf S|)\,d^3x\ge0$, so $M>0$ whenever $q\neq0$ on a set of positive measure.
\item In every frame $M\ge\int E_0\,d^3x$, with equality iff the densities $(u,\mathbf S_{\rm t})(x)$ are all proportional.
\item A plane wave has $M=0$. The waves $E_x=a\cos(kz\mp\omega t)$, $cB_{{\rm t}y}=\pm a\cos(kz\mp\omega t)$ superpose to $E_x=2a\cos kz\cos\omega t$,
$cB_{{\rm t}y}=2a\sin kz\sin\omega t$; per cell (unit area, length $2\pi/k$) $U=2\pi\varepsilon_0a^2/k$ and $\mathbf P=0$, so $Mc^2=U$, although each
constituent has $q_c=0$ and $U_\pm=|\mathbf P_\pm|c$. The total has $q_c=-2a^2(\cos2kz+\cos2\omega t)$, $\int_{\rm cell}T^{zz}=U$,
$\int_{\rm cell}T^{xx}=-U\cos2\omega t$, and the integrated rest energy $\int_{\rm cell}u_0$ oscillates between $\tfrac2\pi U$ and $U$.
\item With $c=1$ and $f(s)=(1-s^2)^2$ on $|s|\le1$, $0$ elsewhere, $E_x=f(z-t)+f(z+t)$, $B_{{\rm t}y}=f(z-t)-f(z+t)$ has, per unit transverse area,
$U=\tfrac{512}{315}$ and $\mathbf P=0$ at all times, so $M=U$, with $(U,P_z)$ a two-vector under boosts along $z$ only; for $|t|\ge1$ the pulses are
disjoint, $q\equiv0$ and $\int E_0=0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(1) Conservation and finite propagation speed make $\int_\Sigma T^{\mu\nu}d\Sigma_\nu$ independent of the spacelike hyperplane $\Sigma$
\cite{Laue1911}; $u-|\mathbf S|=E_0^2/(u+|\mathbf S|)\ge0$, and $M^2=(U-|\mathbf P|)(U+|\mathbf P|)$. (2) Each $(u,\mathbf S_{\rm t})(x)$ is future causal of
length $E_0(x)$ (\cref{d1:prop:rainich}), and future causal vectors obey $|P_1+P_2|\ge|P_1|+|P_2|$, with equality iff proportional. (3) Elementary
cell integrals; for the last statement, with $s=\sin^2\omega t$ one has $q_c=4a^2(s-\cos^2kz)$, so $u_0=2\varepsilon_0a^2|s-\cos^2kz|$, whose mean is
least at the median $s=\tfrac12$ of the arcsine law, giving $\tfrac2\pi U$. (4) $u=f(z-t)^2+f(z+t)^2$ and $S_z=f(z-t)^2-f(z+t)^2$, the cross terms
cancelling, and $q=-4f(z-t)f(z+t)$.
\end{proof}

Items (1) and (3) are inherited, (1) being the statement of Section~14.4 of \cite{ES17}. The cell in (3) is not isolated: $\int T^{zz}=U$ is the
pressure on whatever confines it, so its $(U,\mathbf P)$ is a four-vector only with the walls included, as for radiation in a box \cite{Laue1911}.
Items (2) and (4) refute the reading, proposed in the working dictionary of this investigation, that the mass of a whole ``emerges from the
interference that makes $q\neq0$'': interference suffices for $M>0$ but is not necessary. A whole gets its mass from the non-collinearity of its
constituents' energy--momenta, as two photons do; the interference reading is obstructed. $M$ is also blind to orthogonality, by the example of
Section~14.4 of \cite{ES17}: $(\bE,\Btext)=(0,\mathbf F)$ and $(\mathbf F\sin\vartheta,\mathbf F\cos\vartheta)$ have the same $T^{\mu\nu}$ (d1-6a--d1-6c).

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{The momentum reading of ``$E$ is six-dimensional''}{The momentum reading of "E is six-dimensional"}}

\begin{proposition}[Splitting the temporal energy lowers the mass]
\label{d1:prop:momentum}
Let $P=\sum_i(\mathsf E_i/c)e_i+\sum_ap_ae_{a+3}$ with $\eta(P,P)=\mu^2c^2$, $\widehat W$ the symmetric clock of Proposition~3.5 of \cite{ES17},

$(1,1,1)$, the relative-time energies. The mass read by the clock, $m_4^2c^4:=\mathsf E_W^2-|\bp|^2c^2$, is

m_4^2c^4=\mu^2c^4-|\mathsf E_\perp|^2\le\mu^2c^4 ;
\]
for the clock $e_1$, $m_4^2c^4=\mu^2c^4-\mathsf E_2^2-\mathsf E_3^2$, and $\mathsf E=(3,4,0)$, $\bp=(5,0,0)$, $c=1$ give $\mu=0$, $m_4^2=-16$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$|(\mathsf E_1,\mathsf E_2,\mathsf E_3)|^2=\mathsf E_W^2+|\mathsf E_\perp|^2$ and $\mu^2c^4=c^2\eta(P,P)=|(\mathsf E_1,\mathsf E_2,\mathsf E_3)|^2-|\bp|^2c^2$.
\end{proof}

Obstructed; this restates Proposition~27.1 and Example~27.3 of \cite{ES17} in clock-covariant form (d1-7). In the momentum reading the

\cite{ES17}, $\mathsf E_\perp=0$, nothing is left to differentiate; in the field-strength reading the motion part adds, $E_6\ge E_0$. Only the latter
carries the structure the user describes.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Three candidates for $m$ in the arena's own structures}{Three candidates for m in the arena's own structures}}
\label{d1:sec:m}

\begin{proposition}[The mass form acts on the frame as parity]
\label{d1:prop:Tparity}
Let $T=e_1e_2e_3$ and $P:=\Ad_T|_V$. (1) $P=\eta$ fixes $\bB$ and reverses $\bE$: it is parity on the frame; it commutes with the $L_i$ and
anticommutes with $G$ and the $K_i$, so it normalises $\lG$ without being $\lG$-invariant. (2) $PX$ corresponds to $\bar z$ and $q(z)=h(Pz,z)$, so
$E_6=\tfrac12h(z,z)$ and $E_0=\tfrac12|h(Pz,z)|=\tfrac12\max_\vartheta\eta(e^{\vartheta G}X,e^{\vartheta G}X)$. (3) As a Grassmann mass kernel on spinors $T$ is
invariant under all of $\so{3,3}$, $\lG$ included, in the sense of Definition~18.2 of \cite{ES17}, and breaks nothing; only its sign changes, under
conjugation by a spatial generator $e_4,e_5,e_6$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(1) Lemma~3.4 of \cite{ES17}; $\eta G\eta=-G$ blockwise, the $L_i$ preserve each triple and the $K_i$ exchange them. (2) $z(\eta X)=\bB-i\bE$,
$h(\bar z,z)=\sum_jz_j^2$, and $\eta(e^{\vartheta G}X,e^{\vartheta G}X)=\Re(e^{2i\vartheta}q)$. (3) By Lemma~2.1 of \cite{ES17}, $e_ae_b$ commutes with
$e_1e_2e_3$ when $a,b$ have the same sign and anticommutes otherwise, and $T^{\!\top}=-T$; this is Corollary~18.4 of \cite{ES17}.
\end{proof}

The identities are inherited or restate \cite{ES17} (d1-8a); their consequence for the dictionary is negative, but as a type mismatch:
``$m\leftrightarrow T$'' fails on frame vectors, where the adjoint action of $T$ is parity, while as a kernel on spinors $T$ is $\lG$-invariant, (3).
The Dirac mass lives on the interior reading, which Proposition~12.6 of \cite{ES17} excludes on the same six directions, and even there it breaks
nothing. What survives is a reading: the rest energy is the overlap of a field with its mirror image, and $P$, the arena's splitting into triples, is
the real structure whose stabiliser in $U(3)$ is $U(1)\cdot O(3)$ (\cref{d1:prop:u3}(4)).

\begin{proposition}[The interior energy is not a rest energy]
\label{d1:prop:interior}
For the split-form interior with the binding root (Theorem~16.2 of \cite{ES17}) every solution is $z(\tau)=c_-e^{-i\kappa\tau}+c_+e^{i\kappa\tau}$, and
$\ddot X=-\kappa^2X$ does not involve $m$. The Noether energy $\mathcal E=2m\kappa^2\Re(c_-\cdot c_+)$ (Proposition~16.5(iii) of \cite{ES17}) is indefinite
and vanishes identically on the duality rotations of both senses, $z=e^{-i\kappa\tau}c_-$ (the orbits without momentum, $c_+=0$, of
Proposition~16.5(i)) and $z=e^{i\kappa\tau}c_+$ (which carry the momentum $2i\kappa c_+e^{i\kappa\tau}\neq0$); on both, the frame field's $E_6$ and $E_0$ are
constant. In general
\[
|z|^2=|c_-|^2+|c_+|^2+2\Re\bigl(h(c_-,c_+)e^{2i\kappa\tau}\bigr),\qquad q(z)=e^{-2i\kappa\tau}c_-\cdot c_-+2c_-\cdot c_++e^{2i\kappa\tau}c_+\cdot c_+,
\]
so the frame field's $E_6$ is constant iff $h(c_-,c_+)=0$, and its $E_0$ oscillates: $c_-=e_1$, $c_+=e_2$ give $\mathcal E=0$, $E_6=1$ and
$E_0=|\cos2\kappa\tau|$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With $\eta(GX,GX)=-\eta(X,X)=-\Re q$, $\mathcal E=\tfrac m2\Re(\dot z\cdot\dot z+\kappa^2z\cdot z)=2m\kappa^2\Re(c_-\cdot c_+)$; the momentum is
$\dot z+i\kappa z=2i\kappa c_+e^{i\kappa\tau}$; expand $\bar z\cdot z$ and $z\cdot z$.
\end{proof}

Obstructed as an identification, verified symbolically (d1-9): in the interior $m$ is an overall factor and an input independent of $\kappa$, the
pure duality motion of either sense carries zero energy, and, with $X$ a length and $\kappa=c/\ell$, $\mathcal E=mc^2\cdot2\Re(c_-\cdot c_+)/\ell^2$ has the
form $mc^2$ times a dimensionless amplitude, but the amplitude is indefinite, vanishes on the pure duality orbits, and is selected by nothing, since
$\ell$ is free. The entry ``$c^2\leftrightarrow\kappa G$'' is right that $\kappa G$ lies in the factorising summand of Theorem~17.3 of \cite{ES17}, but
nothing in the interior turns $\kappa^2$ into a rest energy.

\begin{theorem}[In the closure the rest energy of the momentum field breaks the duality translation]
\label{d1:thm:closure}
In the Lie--Poisson closure of Proposition~17.8 of \cite{ES17}, $H=h^2/(2I_0)+\tfrac12p^{\!\top}Np$, with momentum field $\pi=\eta^{-1}p$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item for every $N$, $E_6(\pi)=\tfrac12|p|^2$, $E_0(\pi)=\tfrac12|w(p)|$ and $\mathbf S(\pi)$ are Casimirs of the momentum field (constant already in the
clamped interior, where $p=e^{-\kappa\tau G}p_0$);
\item for Lorentz-class $N=(\nu_1\eta^{-1}+\nu_2G\eta^{-1})/m$ the reduced Hamiltonian is
\[
H=\frac{h^2}{2I_0}+\frac{|\nu_1+i\nu_2|\,E_0(\pi_0)}m\cos(2\theta+\varphi_0),\qquad R_0e^{i\varphi_0}:=(\nu_1+i\nu_2)\,w(p_0),
\]
so the rotor's translation $\theta\mapsto\theta+{\rm const}$, the duality rotation of the momentum field, is broken with amplitude
$|\nu_1+i\nu_2|E_0(\pi_0)/m$; for $(\nu_1,\nu_2)\neq(0,0)$ the rate $\kappa=h/I_0$ is constant and nonzero iff $E_0(\pi_0)=0$ and $h(0)\neq0$
(Corollary~17.14 of \cite{ES17}); for $E_0(\pi_0)>0$ and $(\nu_1,\nu_2)\neq(0,0)$ it is constant only at the equilibria $h(0)=0$, $2\theta(0)+\varphi_0\in\pi\Z$, where it vanishes;
for $(\nu_1,\nu_2)=(0,0)$ it is constant for all data;
\item for the duality-class $N=\id/m$ the potential $\tfrac12p^{\!\top}Np$ (the ``frame energy'' of \cite{ES17}) is $E_6(\pi)/m$, the undivided energy of
the momentum field, and the rate is conserved for all data;
\item the frame field is not conserved: with a free rotor in the rotating regime, Lorentz-class $N$, $(\nu_1,\nu_2)\neq(0,0)$ and $E_0(\pi_0)>0$, the
frame field's $E_6$ and $E_0$ grow without bound.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(1) Along the flow $p=e^{-\theta G}p_0$, so $\pi=e^{\theta G}\pi_0$ and $z(\pi)=e^{i\theta}z(\pi_0)$, a duality rotation, which preserves $E_6$, $E_0$,
$\mathbf S$; and $|p|=|\pi|$ as $\eta$ is orthogonal. (2) is Theorem~17.13 of \cite{ES17}, $R_0=|\nu_1+i\nu_2|\,|w(p_0)|=2|\nu_1+i\nu_2|E_0(\pi_0)$,
and $\dot h=(R_0/m)\sin(2\theta+\varphi_0)$: if $R_0>0$ and $h$ is constant, $\sin(2\theta+\varphi_0)\equiv0$, so $\theta$ is constant and $h=I_0\dot\theta=0$;
conversely these points are equilibria; the rest is Corollary~17.14. (3) $N=\id/m$ commutes with $G$, so $\dot h=-p^{\!\top}GNp=0$. (4) By
Theorem~17.16 of \cite{ES17} the frame $x$ (with $X=e^{-\theta G}x$, so $E_6(X)=E_6(x)$, $E_0(X)=E_0(x)$) is displaced by $\Delta x=\lambda e^{-\varphi_0G}Np_0$,
$\lambda>0$, in each period of the rate, and $x=x_0+n\Delta x$ after $n$ periods, since $\dot\theta$ and $\dot x=e^{\theta G}Ne^{-\theta G}p_0$ depend on
$\theta$ with period $\pi$. Since $G$ acts as $i$, $z(Np_0)=(\nu_1+i\nu_2)z(\pi_0)/m$
and $q(\Delta x)=\lambda^2e^{-2i\varphi_0}(\nu_1+i\nu_2)^2q(\pi_0)/m^2\neq0$, so $|q(x)|$ and $|x|^2$ grow like $n^2$.
\end{proof}

Parts (1)--(3) restate Proposition~17.8, Theorem~17.13 and Corollary~17.14 of \cite{ES17}, fixed there, with $\tfrac12|w|$ renamed $E_0(\pi)$; part (4)
is a direct consequence of Theorem~17.16. Nothing in the theorem is new. Verified by integrating the full system (conservation to $10^{-9}$), at
the exact equilibria $\pi_0=e_1$, $h(0)=0$, $(\nu_1,\nu_2)=(\pm1,0)$, where the rate is constant with $E_0(\pi_0)=\tfrac12$, and with the frame
included, where the frame field's $E_0$ grows as $n^2$ (d1-10a, d1-10b). Read through a dictionary that is interpretive, not a theorem (the
clamped rate $\kappa=c/\ell$, with $\ell$ free, for the user's $c$, and $E_0(\pi)$, which has the dimension of a momentum squared, for a rest energy), the momentum field's rate keeps its symmetry
(constancy, invariance under the rotor's duality translation) when the momentum field is null, its rest part $E_0(\pi)$ is the amplitude that breaks
it, and $E_6(\pi)$ is a Casimir and breaks nothing. But the breaking is explicit, from an inertia that Theorem~17.11 of \cite{ES17} forbids from also
conserving the rate; what is broken is the constancy of the rotor rate, a duality property, not Lorentz invariance and nothing carried by $c$; and with
the undivided energy as Hamiltonian ($N=\id/m$) the rate is constant at the price of the frame drift of Theorem~17.12.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{What transport by $b$ does to the two energies}{What transport by b does to the two energies}}
\label{d1:sec:b}

An admissible Yang--Mills generator is compact: Theorem~2.3 and Corollary~2.4 of \cite{ES17} restrict gauge generators to
$\so3_T\oplus\so3_S\subset\so{3,3}$, the rotations of the two triples. Write such a $b$ as $(A+D,A-D)$, $A+D$ acting on the temporal triple and $A-D$ on the
spatial one; the diagonal part $(A,A)$ lies in $\lG$ and the anti-diagonal part $(D,-D)$ in $\mathfrak p_G$. Boost-valued connections are legitimate
metric connections (Proposition~16.7 of \cite{ES17}) but not admissible Yang--Mills potentials.

\begin{proposition}[What transport preserves]
\label{d1:prop:bfield}
(1) $Y\in\so{3,3}$ preserves $\bE\cdot\bB$ for all $X$, equivalently $q$, equivalently $E_0$, iff $[Y,G]=0$, that is $Y\in\lG$. (2) A compact
$b=(A+D,A-D)$ preserves $E_6$ and $\Re q=|\bB|^2-|\bE|^2$; it preserves $E_0$ for all $X$ iff $D=0$; the anti-diagonal rotation through $\tfrac\pi4$
about $e_3$ carries the null field $(1,i,0)$ to the field $\tfrac{1+i}{\sqrt2}(1,1,0)$ at rest, $E_0$ going from $0$ to $1$ at $E_6=1$. (3) At three
constituents the part of $\mathfrak{ps}_W$ commuting with $G_3$, $\so{4}_\Delta\cong\su{2}\oplus\su{2}$ of Theorem~17.21 of \cite{ES17}, consists of paired
rotations, so it preserves the $\so{9,\C}$ invariants $\tfrac12|Z|^2=\tfrac12\sum_k|z^{(k)}|^2$ and $q_3(Z)=\sum_kz^{(k)}\cdot z^{(k)}$, hence $\tfrac12|q_3|$.
(4) It preserves neither the constituents' rest energies nor that of their summed density: the rotation through $\pi/4$ generated by
$\tfrac12\mathrm{rot}(7,13)$ maps $(z^{(2)},z^{(3)})=((1,i,0),(1,0,0))$ to $((0,i,0),(\sqrt2,0,0))$, keeping $\tfrac12|Z|^2=\tfrac32$ and $q_3=1$, while
$\sum_kE_0^{(k)}$ goes from $\tfrac12$ to $\tfrac32$ and $(\sum_kE_6^{(k)};\sum_k\mathbf S_{\rm t}^{(k)})$ from $(\tfrac32;0,0,1)$ to $(\tfrac32;0,0,0)$, its
Lorentz length from $\tfrac{\sqrt5}2$ to $\tfrac32$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(1) For $Y\in\so{3,3}$, $Y^{\!\top}\eta G+\eta GY=\eta[G,Y]$. If $Y$ preserves $\Re q$ and $|q|$, then $\Im q\cdot\delta(\Im q)=0$, so $\delta(\Im q)=0$ since
polynomials form an integral domain. (2) $b$ rotates each triple orthogonally, so it preserves $|\bB|^2$ and $|\bE|^2$; $(A,A)$ commutes with $G$ and
$G(D,-D)G^{-1}=(-D,D)$, so by (1) $E_0$ is preserved for all $X$ iff $D=0$; the example is $\bB=(1,0,0)\mapsto\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,1,0)$,
$\bE=(0,1,0)\mapsto\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,1,0)$. (3) By Theorem~17.19 of \cite{ES17} the elements of $\so4_\Delta$ are paired rotations, with $S=0$ and $R$ real
antisymmetric, hence they preserve $|Z|^2$ and $q_3$. (4) Direct, with $[e_Ae_B,e_C]=2\eta_{BC}e_A-2\eta_{AC}e_B$, checked against the realisation.
\end{proof}

Verified exactly (d1-11a, d1-11b). Parts (1) and (2) are inherited linear algebra, and they cut both ways. Pointwise, the anti-diagonal part of an
admissible $b$ does turn a null field into one at rest at fixed undivided energy, while the diagonal part, a rotation in $\lG$, changes no invariant.
But the anti-diagonal part is the part that fails to commute with $G$: along a worldline it cannot be gauged away, unlike the diagonal part
(Larmor's theorem \cite{Larmor1897}), and in the interior it produces Jordan blocks and secular growth unless screened by a diagonal part at equal rates, outside one
exceptional ratio (\cref{d4:thm:screen});
nothing here selects an amount of rest energy. Parts (3)--(4) are an obstruction for the $\so{9,\C}$ invariants only: no
transport valued in the paired rotations changes $\tfrac12|Z|^2$ or $q_3$. They are not one for the rest energy of the whole in the diagonal Lorentz
reading, which the example changes, and $\mathrm{rot}(7,13)$ does not commute with the diagonal rotations; which is physical depends on the Lorentz
action at three constituents, which the paper leaves open (Table~7 of \cite{ES17}; at three constituents the closure symmetry meets the
interior Lorentz algebra of the first constituent in $0$, Theorem~17.20 there); and $\so4_\Delta$ is the part of $\mathfrak{ps}_W$ commuting with $G_3$,
not the only part with bounded orbits (\cref{d4:sec:bfield}). Here $b$ appears only as a constant transport element or a holonomy, with no field
strength or $b$-field energy, so ``where the $b$-field belongs'' is, at this level, interpretive.
